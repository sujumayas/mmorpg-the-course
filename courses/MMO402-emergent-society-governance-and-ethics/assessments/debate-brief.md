## What this component is for

The course outcome behind the debate is "argue ethical positions on world operation". Arguing well in a studio means finding the exact point where a decision flips and saying whether that point is a fact or a value. The debate tests that skill in public, against a prepared opponent, on a question this course has already modelled.

The audience vote decides who wins on the day. It does not decide your grade. A team that loses the vote with a clear crux and a fair steelman can earn full marks.

## Format

Each debate is Oxford-style: one motion, a proposition team and an opposition team of three, and an audience vote before and after.

| Stage | Time | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Audience vote on the motion | 2 min | Audience |
| Opening speeches | 5 min each | Proposition 1, then Opposition 1 |
| Cross-examination | 3 min each | Opposition questions Proposition 1, then the reverse |
| Second speeches | 5 min each | Proposition 2, then Opposition 2 |
| Floor questions | 8 min | Audience to either team |
| Closing speeches | 3 min each | Opposition 3, then Proposition 3 |
| Audience vote again | 2 min | Audience |

Two rules from the lecture are part of the format and are checked by the markers.

- **The first opening speech must state the crux as the speaker's team sees it.** That means the criterion, the crux weight or crux score, and whether the team thinks the disagreement is one of fact or of value.
- **The first opposition speech must say whether it accepts that crux or relocates it.** Relocating means naming a different criterion, weight or fact on which the motion turns, with a reason.

Sides are assigned by coin toss 48 hours before the debate. You prepare both sides. Timekeeping is strict: the chair allows 15 seconds over, then stops the speaker.

Every team member gives one of the three speeches. Proposition 1 and Opposition 1 also answer cross-examination. Any member may answer floor questions.

## Deliverables

1. **The debate case file**, as specified in [lab 14](../labs/lab14.md), submitted by one member for the team at the start of the debate. It must contain the criteria table with constraints listed separately, the script output, the decision map with both sides placed, three Toulmin arguments, two steelmen, the evidence log and the "Our crux" paragraph. It must work for either side.
2. **The debate itself.** All three members speak.
3. **A contribution statement**, one line per member, signed by all three, saying who prepared which parts of the case file. Submit it with the case file.

## How you are marked

The debate is marked out of 100 on the four criteria in week 14, section 5, with equal weight. Each criterion is worth 25 points. Two markers score independently and agree a mark. The team mark applies to every member, subject to the contribution rule below.

The markers judge each criterion on the debate itself. They use the case file as evidence of preparation, to check a crux the speakers stated in passing, or to see where a piece of evidence came from. A team that submits no case file cannot score above the Adequate band on criteria 1 and 2, because the markers cannot check the crux calculation or the evidence log.

### Rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Points | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak or missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Identifying and stating the crux | 25 | 22-25: the opening speech states the crux with its number (a crux weight or crux score) and labels it fact or value. The first opposition speech explicitly accepts or relocates it with a reason. The team returns to the crux in the closing speech. | 16-21: crux stated and labelled, but its number is missing or wrong, or the closing does not return to it | 10-15: a crux is implied but never stated as the point on which the motion turns, or it is mislabelled as fact or value | 0-9: no crux, or the team argues around the question instead of at it |
| 2. Quality of evidence and fit to its axis | 25 | 22-25: evidence is sourced with version or date, each item is used on the axis it can answer (telemetry and comparable cases for facts, principles and polls of stated populations for values), and its typical flaw is acknowledged when an opponent could exploit it | 16-21: good evidence, with one item used on the wrong axis (for example a poll offered as proof of what a policy did) | 10-15: evidence mostly relevant but thinly sourced, or several items used on the wrong axis | 0-9: assertion without evidence, or real-game facts that are wrong or unsourced |
| 3. A fair steelman of the other side | 25 | 22-25: before rebutting, the team states the other side's strongest case so well that the other side would accept it, and names what it agrees with | 16-21: a fair statement of the other side, but not its strongest version | 10-15: the other side is stated, but in a weakened or loaded form | 0-9: no steelman, or a straw man |
| 4. Rebuttal that engages the strongest point | 25 | 22-25: rebuttal targets the other side's strongest argument, usually its warrant at the crux, and shows with a number or a principle why it fails or where it holds | 16-21: rebuttal engages a strong point but not the strongest, or engages the strongest without evidence | 10-15: rebuttal mostly answers weak or peripheral points | 0-9: no rebuttal, or rebuttal of arguments nobody made |

The audience votes are recorded and reported with the marks. They do not enter the grade. Neither does the result of the coin toss: markers are told which side each team drew and do not reward or penalize the side itself.

### Contribution rule

Every member receives the team mark if the contribution statement shows a fair share of the work and the member gave a speech. If the statement and the tutor's observation in the week 14 lab agree that one member contributed much less, the tutor may lower that member's mark by one band on criteria 2 and 3, the two that depend most on preparation. A member absent from the debate without a documented reason scores 0. With a documented reason, that member instead submits a written steelman and rebuttal of 800 words on the same motion within one week, marked on criteria 3 and 4 and scaled to 100.

## What a strong debate looks like

- The first proposition speaker says something like: "We think this motion turns on the weight you give to protecting players from loss against the freedom to betray. On our scores, the motion passes for anyone who puts more than 0.55 on protection. That is a value question, and we will argue it as one."
- The first opposition speaker either accepts that and argues the weight, or relocates it: "We think it turns on a fact. Do permissive scam rules drive away new players? If they do not, the motion fails on the proposition's own weights."
- Evidence arrives with its axis named. "This is a poll. It tells you what voters wanted, not what the policy did."
- Each team states the other side's case fairly before attacking it.

## Academic integrity

Discuss the motion widely. Everything in the case file must be your team's own words. Cite every real-game fact with its version or date, as the course's lectures do. Do not introduce a game fact you cannot source. If you use a generative AI tool to find sources or check arithmetic, say so in the case file and verify every source it gives you yourself.
