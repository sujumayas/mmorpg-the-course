Same rate, different worst case

All three randomizers deal the I piece once in seven, and the lanes look equally random. The histogram below shows what differs. Only the 7-bag has a wall: it never makes you wait more than 12 pieces.

Algorithms follow the lecture: pure 1-in-7, the NES-style reroll rule as documented on the Tetris Wiki, and a Fisher-Yates 7-bag. Each randomizer has its own mulberry32 generator seeded with the seed above. JavaScript cannot reproduce Python's Mersenne Twister, so the exact longest droughts differ from the lecture's Python table for seed 13. The averages, rates and the bag's 12-piece wall match. Pieces are drawn as plain lettered squares.
