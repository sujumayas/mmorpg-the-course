# GD101 · Playtest reports brief

**Weight:** 30% of the course grade, as three reports worth 10% each. Each report is marked out of 100.
**Work mode:** written individually. You may run sessions with your prototype team, but each person submits their own report, with their own logs as the appendix.

## The three reports

The three core reports come from the three labs that teach the playtest and iteration method in sequence. Each builds on the one before.

| Report | Lab | Assigned | Due | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Lab 07, silent playtest and observation log](../labs/lab07.md) | Week 7 | Start of week 8 | You can run a silent session and turn logs into ranked findings |
| 2 | [Lab 08, two-loop iteration sprint](../labs/lab08.md) | Week 8 | Start of week 9 | You can set kill criteria before testing and decide honestly |
| 3 | [Lab 09, solve it, then fix it](../labs/lab09.md) | Week 9 | Start of week 10 | You can audit decisions and test a rule change aimed at a dead one |

**Replacement reports.** Labs [02](../labs/lab02.md), [11](../labs/lab11.md) and [12](../labs/lab12.md) also produce short reports in playtest format. Each is marked with the rubric below. The highest of the three can replace your lowest core report mark. This gives you a second chance on the method after feedback, and it rewards the balance and readability work of weeks 11 and 12. A replacement report cannot fill in for a core report you did not submit.

## What every report contains

The lab file sets the exact tasks. Every report also meets these common standards.

1. **Test goals first.** One to three questions a session can answer, written before recruiting.
2. **Tester profiles** without names: how often each plays board or card games, and whether they had seen your game.
3. **Method statement.** Protocol used, how many sessions, and a section headed "Where I broke the rule" listing every explanation, leading prompt or ASSIST and what it may have hidden. Zero rule breaks is possible but rare; say how you know.
4. **Findings list** ranked by severity, then count. Counts are written "k of n testers", never as percentages of players (week 7, section 2).
5. **The lab's own analysis.** For report 1, the coverage check. For report 2, the loop measurement and semester plan. For report 3, the solver output, decision audit and fix test.
6. **Decision and next step.** What you will change, keep or cut, and the question for the next loop.
7. **Appendix.** Raw observation logs (photos are fine), one sheet per session.

Length limits are set in each lab (two to four pages plus the appendix). Pages past the limit are not read.

## Rubric (per report, 100 points)

The lab file's rubric lists the lab-specific criteria. This table sets the points. "Lab-specific analysis" covers the lab's own task and uses the lab rubric's criteria for that task.

| Criterion | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Session method** (20) | 18-20: Protocol followed as written; intro script and consent used; only neutral prompts; every rule break reported with what it hid | 14-17: Minor slips, all reported | 10-13: Slips found in the logs but not reported, or protocol partly followed | 0-9: Rules explained during play, or no evidence of the protocol |
| **Observation quality** (20) | 18-20: Timed observations with quotes; interpretation kept in its own column | 14-17: Mostly observations, a few conclusions in the "what happened" column | 10-13: Mix of observations and conclusions throughout | 0-9: Mostly conclusions ("confused by X", "liked it") |
| **Findings** (15) | 14-15: Clustered correctly; counts and severity justified by log references; problems kept apart from fixes | 11-13: Findings sound, evidence thin for one or two | 8-10: Findings mixed with fixes, or counts missing | 0-7: Fewer than four findings, or not supported by the logs |
| **Lab-specific analysis** (25) | 23-25: Every calculation correct with working; model assumptions stated; conclusions follow from the numbers | 18-22: One calculation error that does not change the conclusion | 13-17: Several errors, or numbers present without being used | 0-12: Missing or wrong |
| **Decision and next step** (10) | 9-10: Decision follows from the evidence and any criteria set before testing; next question is testable | 7-8: Decision plausible, link to evidence loose | 5-6: Decision stated without reasons | 0-4: Decision contradicts the evidence, or missing |
| **Writing** (10) | 9-10: Numbers first, plain sentences, within the page limit | 7-8: Readable but padded | 5-6: Hard to find the findings | 0-4: Over the limit or hard to follow |

## How reports are checked

- The tutor compares a sample of findings with your raw logs. A finding with no matching log line is marked as unsupported.
- Invented or altered data scores 0 for the report and is treated as academic misconduct.
- Reports from the same team may share session data, but the findings, analysis and decision must be your own writing. State in the method section which sessions you facilitated and which you observed.
