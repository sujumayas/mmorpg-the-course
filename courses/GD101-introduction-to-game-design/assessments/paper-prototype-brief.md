# GD101 · Paper prototype brief

**Weight:** 40% of the course grade, marked out of 100.
**Team:** two or three students. A solo project needs your tutor's approval by the end of week 5.
**Final submission:** the week 15 showcase, with the written pack due at the start of your showcase slot.

## What you are making

You design, build and iterate one paper game over weeks 5 to 15. It can be a board game, a card game, or a paper model of a digital game's system with one person playing "the computer" (week 6, section 1). It must be playable in 20 to 40 minutes by two to five people who have never seen it, using only your rules sheet.

The mark rewards evidence of design method more than polish. A plain game with a clear experience goal, honest playtest data and well-reasoned cuts will beat a beautiful game with no record of how it got there. Week 6 warns that polish makes you reluctant to change things. Keep components cheap until the rules settle.

## Deliverables

Everything below is due at the start of your week 15 showcase slot, in one folder (physical kit plus a PDF pack).

1. **The kit.** All components, labelled with the final version number, in a box or envelope, with a setup photo taped inside the lid.
2. **Rules sheet.** At most two sides of A4, numbered, versioned and dated. A stranger must be able to set up and play from it.
3. **One-page design** in the lab 14 format, with the pillar-mechanic grid, swap test and change log on a second page ([lab 14](../labs/lab14.md)).
4. **Iteration log.** One entry per loop from week 6 onward in the lab 08 format, each with kill criteria dated before the test ([lab 08](../labs/lab08.md)). Expect at least five loops.
5. **Design evidence appendix.** The experience goals page (lab 05), the dice-or-deck decision note (lab 06), the luck audit and upset rates (lab 10), a decision audit of every choice point (week 9, section 3) and a payoff table or extreme test for your strongest option (week 11).
6. **Showcase summary** (one page, handed in within 48 hours of the showcase). Findings from your two showcase sessions, ranked by severity and count as in week 7 section 5, and one sentence per experience goal saying whether the showcase evidence supports it.
7. **Contribution statement** (one paragraph per team member, signed by all). Who did what, with lab references.

## The week 15 showcase

Each team runs its game at a table for two 30-minute sessions. The players are classmates from other teams and invited guests.

- Players read your one-pager for two minutes, then play from the rules sheet. You follow the week 7 protocol: read the intro script, use only neutral prompts, log observations with times and quotes, and log any ASSIST with your exact words.
- An assessor watches part of one session without speaking to the players.
- After the second session you have a five-minute viva with the assessor. The assessor picks two "apply" questions from the [exam bank](exam-bank.md) and asks them about your game. For example, "Build the payoff table for your two strongest openings" or "Which pillar is fragile, and what would the swap test say about your last cut?" Every team member should be ready to answer. The viva prompts at the end of the exam bank show the pattern.

## Rubric for the final (60 points)

| Criterion | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Playability from the sheet** (15) | 14-15: Showcase players set up and finish a game from the rules sheet; no ASSIST, rules questions are about strategy, not procedure | 11-13: Game finished; one or two procedural questions or one ASSIST, each logged | 8-10: Game finished with several ASSISTs, or the rules sheet needed a spoken summary | 0-7: Players could not finish without the team explaining |
| **Experience goals met** (10) | 9-10: Each goal checked against showcase observations with counts; the showcase summary says plainly which goals held and which did not | 7-8: Goals checked, evidence thin for one goal | 5-6: Goals mentioned but checked by impression ("people seemed to like it") | 0-4: No link between goals and showcase evidence |
| **Decisions and balance** (10) | 9-10: Decision audit covers every choice point; strongest option tested with a payoff table or extreme test; luck level matched to the stated audience with measured upset rates | 7-8: One of the three analyses is thin | 5-6: Analyses present but not used to change the game | 0-4: Missing, or a known dominant strategy left unaddressed without a reason |
| **Iteration record** (10) | 9-10: At least five loops; kill criteria dated before each test; at least one honest kill; change log matches the log entries | 7-8: Four or more loops; one set of criteria vague or undated | 5-6: Loops present but decisions not tied to criteria | 0-4: Log written after the fact or fewer than three loops |
| **Feedback and readability** (5) | 5: Every state change has a physical action; key information uses two channels; one-second glance test passes with showcase players | 4: One gap | 3: Several state changes still happen "in players' heads" | 0-2: Players repeatedly misread the state |
| **Viva** (10) | 9-10: Both answers apply the model to your game with your own numbers and say what the model misses | 7-8: One answer strong, one general | 5-6: Answers recall the lecture without applying it | 0-4: Cannot connect the model to the game |

## Team marks and individual adjustment

The team receives one mark. The tutor may move an individual's mark up or down by up to 10 points when the contribution statement, lab attendance and lab records show a clear imbalance. The tutor talks to the team before making any adjustment.

## Rules that apply to every component

- **Data must be real.** Every count in your log and reports must come from a session that happened. Invented or "tidied" data scores 0 for the component and is treated as academic misconduct.
- **Testers must consent.** Use the lab 07 consent line. Do not name testers in anything you submit.
- **No copied art or rules.** Use your own drawings or plain text. If your game adapts a public-domain game, say so on the one-pager.
