# GD101 · Participation brief

**Weight:** 10% of the course grade, marked out of 100.
**Work mode:** individual, recorded weekly by your lab tutor.

## Why participation is graded

Design is learned at the table. Most of this course's evidence comes from people playing each other's prototypes, so a student who skips sessions weakens other teams' data as well as their own. Participation marks the work that keeps the course running: in-class activities, the short labs that practise a model before it matters, testing other teams' games, and the showcase.

Participation is marked on what you hand in and what you do, never on how often you speak. Quiet students who do the work get full marks.

## What counts

| Part | What you do | Points |
|---|---|---|
| In-class activities | Hand in the "expected output" named in each lecture's in-class activity, weeks 1 to 14. Two points each: 2 for complete, 1 for partial, 0 for missing | 28 |
| Practice labs | Labs [01](../labs/lab01.md), [03](../labs/lab03.md), [04](../labs/lab04.md) and [05](../labs/lab05.md) part B and C. Each is marked with its lab rubric and scaled to 8 points | 32 |
| Testing for other teams | Act as a silent tester or observer for another team's prototype, weeks 7 to 12. Each session counts once the other team's log records you (by initials) and you hand in a three-line note: date, team, one observation you would want as the designer. 5 points per session, up to 4 sessions | 20 |
| Showcase feedback | In week 15, play at least three other teams' prototypes and fill in the showcase feedback form for each (one finding stated as an observation, one question for the designers). Three forms earns full points | 10 |
| Weekly quizzes | Complete the start-of-lab quiz, drawn from the [exam bank](exam-bank.md), in weeks 2 to 14. One point per quiz for a genuine attempt, up to 10. The quiz score is feedback only | 10 |

Total: 100.

## Rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Full | Partial | Minimal | None |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **In-class activities** (28) | 25-28: At least 13 outputs, complete and matching the task | 17-24: 9 to 12 outputs, or several partial | 9-16: 5 to 8 outputs | 0-8: Fewer than 5 |
| **Practice labs** (32) | 29-32: All four labs at "excellent" or "adequate" on most rows | 20-28: All four handed in, one weak | 10-19: Two or three handed in | 0-9: One or none |

| **Weekly quizzes** (10) | 10: 10 or more attempts | 6-9: 6 to 9 attempts | 1-5: 1 to 5 attempts | 0: None |

## Absences

If you miss a session for a documented reason, your tutor will set a short make-up task: the week's in-class activity done alone, or one exam-bank question per missed quiz. Testing sessions can be made up in any lab from week 7 to week 12. The showcase has no make-up session. If you miss it for a documented reason, you play three prototypes at a make-up table in the exam period.

## Being a good tester

The testing you do for other teams follows the same rules as your own playtests (week 7). Play from the rules sheet. Think aloud. Do not ask the designers to explain; if you get stuck, say what you are thinking. Your honest confusion is the most useful thing you can give another team. A tester who "helps" by guessing what the designer meant hides the problem from them.
