# GD101 · Lab 01: Make it a game

**Week:** 1 · **Time:** 2 hours in the lab plus up to 2 hours at home · **Weight:** part of the participation component (10%). Not one of the three core playtest reports, which come from labs 07 to 09

**Lecture:** [Week 01, What is a game?](../lectures/w01-what-is-a-game.md)

## Objective

Take an everyday activity that is not a game, add Juul's missing features one at a time, and playtest each version. By the end you will have seen, with real players, which added feature changed behaviour the most, and you will have scored every version against Suits, Salen & Zimmerman and Juul.

## Starter materials

- The seven-column feature checklist from the lecture (F1 rules, F2 variable measurable outcome, F3 valued outcomes, F4 effort matters, F5 attachment, F6 negotiable consequences, S rules forbid the efficient route) and the required-feature table for the three definitions.
- A starting activity. Pick one from this list, or propose your own to the lab tutor:
  - Tossing a paper ball into a bin.
  - Stacking coins.
  - Walking from the lab door to the window.
  - Humming a tune.
  - Sorting a shuffled deck of cards by suit.
- Common props available in the lab: paper, tape, a timer (phone), a pack of playing cards, two six-sided dice, coins, a waste-paper bin.

## Steps

1. **Score v0 (10 min).** Write your chosen activity's seven-column row. Apply the three definitions. It must fail at least two of them; if not, pick a different activity.
2. **Plan the additions (10 min).** List the missing columns in the order you will add them. For each, write the smallest rule that adds that feature. Example for coin stacking: F3 "the goal is a stack of 10", F2 "count coins before it falls", S "only your non-dominant hand".
3. **v1: add one feature (15 min).** Add only the first rule. Recruit two or three classmates from another group as testers. Explain the activity in one sentence, then stay silent and watch for 5 minutes. Write down what they do, say and ask. Do not coach.
4. **v2 and v3 (40 min).** Add the next rule, test with new testers if possible, and log. Repeat once more. By v3 the activity must pass at least two of the three definitions.
5. **Break it (10 min).** Ask a tester to find the easiest way to "win" v3 that you did not intend. Log the exploit and whether it breaks F2, F4 or S.
6. **Reflect (at home).** Write the reflection described under Deliverable.

## Deliverable

One document with:
- The completed version log (v0 to v3, plus the exploit).
- Observation notes from each playtest: at least five dated, specific observations per version ("Tester 2 counted aloud after v2", not "they liked it").
- A 300-word reflection answering: Which single added feature changed tester behaviour the most, and what evidence shows it? Which definition's verdict matched your testers' sense of "now it's a game"? What would you add in a v4?

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent (4) | Adequate (2-3) | Missing or wrong (0-1) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feature scoring | Every version scored in all seven columns; verdicts follow the required-feature table exactly; judgment calls explained | Scoring complete with one or two unexplained judgment calls | Columns missing or verdicts contradict the table | 25% |
| One change per version | Each version adds exactly one rule tied to a named column | One version bundles two changes | Changes not tied to columns | 15% |
| Observation quality | Specific, behavioural notes; no coaching during tests | Notes mix observation and opinion | Only opinions ("fun", "boring") | 25% |
| Exploit | Exploit found, logged and linked to a column | Exploit found, no column named | No exploit attempt | 10% |
| Reflection | Uses evidence from notes to answer all three questions in 250-350 words | Answers questions with little evidence | Generic or missing | 25% |
