## Objective

Write a complete formal-elements sheet for a simple traditional card game, change exactly one formal element, and playtest both versions. You will measure what the change did to play using counts you record at the table, not impressions.

## Starter materials

- One standard 52-card deck per group of four, a timer, and pencils.
- The starter game, **Crazy Eights (lab version)**, written out in full below. It is a traditional public-domain game; these are this course's own lab rules.
- The formal-elements worksheet template below.
- The playtest tally sheet below.

### Crazy Eights (lab version)

- **Players:** 2 to 5, each playing for themselves.
- **Setup:** Shuffle. Deal 7 cards each (5 each with 5 players). Turn the top card of the remaining deck face up to start a discard pile. The rest is the draw pile.
- **Progression:** Play goes clockwise. On your turn, play one card onto the discard pile that matches the top card's suit or rank. Any 8 may be played at any time; when you play an 8, name a suit, and the next player must match that suit (or play another 8). If you cannot or choose not to play, draw one card. If the drawn card can be played, you may play it immediately; otherwise your turn ends.
- **Resolution:** The first player to empty their hand wins. If the draw pile runs out, shuffle all but the top discard to form a new draw pile.

## Steps

1. **Teardown (25 min).** Fill in the worksheet for the lab version. Write the constitutive rules without the words "card", "suit" or "eight": for example, "items with two attributes; a move is legal if it shares an attribute with the last item, or is a wild item". Play one game while filling in the implicit rules (for example, "you may not look at the draw pile", "you must say when you have one card left" if your group assumes it).
2. **Baseline playtest (25 min).** Play three games of the lab version with four players. Fill in one tally row per game.
3. **Change one element (15 min).** Pick exactly one of these changes, or propose another to the tutor. Write a second worksheet for the new version and mark the changed rows.
   - **Player structure:** two teams of two, partners sit opposite, and a team wins when either partner empties their hand.
   - **Sequencing:** each round, all players choose a card face down, then reveal together in seat order; illegal reveals return to hand and that player draws one.
   - **Objective:** the winner is whoever holds the lowest point total (face cards 10, aces 1, others face value) when the first player empties their hand.
   - **Rule:** 8s are no longer wild.
4. **Prediction (5 min).** Before playing, write one sentence predicting how each tally column will change, with a direction (up, down, same).
5. **Variant playtest (30 min).** Play three games of the new version. Fill in the tally rows.
6. **Compare (at home).** Compute the mean of each tally column for both versions. Compare against your predictions.

## Deliverable

A playtest report (PDF, 2 to 3 pages) with:
- Both worksheets, with the changed rows highlighted.
- The six tally rows and the column means for each version.
- Your predictions and whether each came true.
- A 400-word analysis: which formal element did your change touch, which other elements shifted as a result (use the lecture's section 3 as a guide), and would you keep the change? One paragraph must name an implicit rule that caused a question or dispute and propose an operational rule that would settle it.
