# GD101 · Lab 12: Feedback and readability pass

**Week:** 12 · **Time:** 2 hours in the lab plus up to 2 hours at home · **Weight:** optional replacement playtest report. This lab produces one short report in playtest format; the best of the reports from labs 02, 11 and 12 can replace your lowest core report mark (see the [playtest reports brief](../assessments/playtest-reports-brief.md))

**Lecture:** [Week 12, Feedback: juice, clarity, readability](../lectures/w12-feedback-juice-clarity-readability.md)

## Objective

Make your paper prototype easier to read and harder to misplay. You will audit every state change for the three jobs of feedback, measure readability with outside players, and redesign one component so the information players need most is the easiest thing to see.

## Starter materials

- Your team's paper prototype in its current iteration, set up in a typical mid-game position. Photograph that position; you will reuse it.
- A phone timer.
- Blank index cards, markers in at least three colors, and a pencil for shape icons.
- Two or more people from outside your team who have not played your prototype.
- The audit table below (copy into your report).

**Feedback audit table.**

| State change (what changes in the game) | Triggered by | Confirm? How | Report? How | Forecast? How | Physical action at the table |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Readability test sheet.**

| Tester | Glance test answer (who is winning?) | Correct? | Squint test: first thing noticed | Signals misread during play |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Steps

1. **List every state change (20 minutes).** Walk through one full turn of your game slowly. Every time anything about the game state changes (a score, a resource, a position, a rule that switches on, a hidden card becoming known), add a row to the audit table. Include changes that happen "in your head", like a rule that starts applying from round 5.
2. **Fill the jobs (15 minutes).** For each row, write how the game confirms, reports and forecasts it, or write "none". Mark every row whose last column is empty: a state change with no physical action is a change players will forget.
3. **Glance test (15 minutes).** Show your mid-game setup (the real table, or the photo at full screen) to each outside tester for one second, then cover it. Ask: "Who is winning, and how can you tell?" Record the answer and whether it matches the actual state.
4. **Squint test (5 minutes).** Ask each tester to squint or step back two meters from the table and name the first three things they notice. Compare with what a player needs most at this moment in the game. Write down any mismatch.
5. **Observe a short game (25 minutes).** Teach the rules to the outside testers the way you did in week 7, then watch them play for 20 minutes without helping. Log every moment a tester misreads a signal, forgets a rule tied to one of your marked rows, or asks "what just happened?"
6. **Redesign one component (at home).** Pick the card, board area or tracker that caused the most misreads. Redesign it for hierarchy (the most-needed number is largest and in a corner visible when held or fanned), one meaning per signal (no color or icon used for two meanings), and redundancy (every key distinction carried by at least two of color, shape, position and text). Also give at least two of your marked rows a physical action: a token, a track, a flipped card.
7. **Retest (at home).** Repeat the glance test and a 20-minute observed game with at least two new outside testers. Record the same sheets.

## Deliverable

A playtest report of up to 2 pages containing:

- The completed feedback audit table with the rows that lacked a physical action marked.
- Readability test sheets from before and after the redesign.
- Photos or scans of the component before and after, with one sentence per change naming the principle it applies (hierarchy, one meaning per signal, consistency, redundancy).
- A 200-word reflection: which change reduced misreads the most, what the glance test results were before and after, and one feedback problem you chose not to fix yet and why.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent (4) | Adequate (2-3) | Missing or wrong (0-1) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feedback audit | Every state change in a full turn listed; each job filled or marked "none"; missing physical actions identified | Most changes listed; some jobs unassessed | Fewer than half the changes, or no job analysis | 25% |
| Readability testing | Glance and squint tests with 2 or more outside testers, before and after; misreads logged during observed play | Tests run before or after only, or with one tester | No outside testers | 25% |
| Redesign | Component redesigned with all three principles visibly applied and at least two state changes given physical actions | Redesign applies one or two principles, or only one new physical action | No redesign | 30% |
| Reflection | Results compared with numbers (glance-test correct answers, misread counts); honest about what remains | Reflection without before-and-after comparison | Missing | 20% |
