# GD101 · Lab 06: Prototype v0.1 and the dice-or-deck test

**Week:** 6, paper prototyping ([lecture](../lectures/w06-paper-prototyping-cards-dice-boards.md)).
**Time:** 2 hours in lab, plus up to 3 hours at home.
**Weight:** first milestone of the paper prototype (40%). The prototype you build here is the one you test silently in [lab 07](lab07.md).

## Objective

Build version 0.1 of your course paper prototype, playable by strangers from the rules sheet alone. Then run a controlled comparison: the same game with its main random element as dice and as a deck with the same odds. Measure droughts and spread, and decide which to keep with reference to your week 5 experience goals.

## Starter materials

**Kit (per student).** 60 blank index cards, card sleeves plus one old deck of playing cards to back them, two six-sided dice, 40 tokens (coins, beans or chips), an A3 sheet for a board if you need one, pencil, eraser, scissors.

**From earlier weeks.** Your lab 05 goals page (aesthetic profile and three experience goals). If you don't have a concept, use the fallback below.

**Templates to copy into your notebook.**

1. *Design question and goal.* One line each: "This prototype answers: ..." and "Experience goal: ..."
2. *Component list.*

   | Component | How many | Formal element it represents | Why it's needed for the question |
   |---|---|---|---|

3. *Rules sheet v0.1.* At most one side of A4. Sections: Goal of the game; Setup (numbered); A turn (numbered steps); How the game ends; Who wins. Version number and date in the corner.
4. *Changelog.*

   | Version | Change | Why | What happened |
   |---|---|---|---|

5. *Randomizer log.*

   | Game | Version (dice or deck) | Player | Productive turns | Longest dry spell | One-sentence feeling |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Fallback concept, "Floodbridge" (use it only if you have no concept).** Two players each build a bridge of 8 planks before the river floods. Each turn the active player generates materials with a randomizer. Each player owns 3 of 6 "banks," numbered by the randomizer results 2 to 12 (choose them in a draft at the start, one at a time, alternating). Every bank whose number comes up gives its owner 1 wood. Two wood buys a plank. A result of 7 raises the flood marker by 1, and at flood 6 the game ends. The player with more planks wins, and 8 planks wins at once. The randomizer is the element you test.

**Dice-to-deck conversion.** To turn any dice roll into a deck with the same odds, make one card for each equally likely outcome. Two six-sided dice summed give 36 cards: one 2, two 3s, three 4s, four 5s, five 6s, six 7s, five 8s, four 9s, three 10s, two 11s, one 12. A single d6 gives 6 cards. Decide how many cards stay unseen at the bottom before a reshuffle. Catan's event deck leaves 5 of 36 unseen; start there.

## Steps

1. **Question and goal (10 min).** Write your design question and the one experience goal it serves.
2. **Components (15 min).** Fill the component list. Cut anything that doesn't serve the question. Target no more than 6 component types.
3. **Rules v0.1 (25 min).** Write the rules sheet. A stranger must be able to play from it with no help from you, because in lab 07 you're not allowed to explain.
4. **Solo test (15 min).** Play every seat yourself for at least 5 turns. List every rules hole (a situation the rules don't cover, a turn where nobody can act, a way the game fails to end). Fix them and bump the version to v0.1.1.
5. **Pick the random element (5 min).** Name your game's main randomizer and the outcomes that count as "productive" for a player (for example, "any of my three bank numbers comes up").
6. **Randomizer-only runs (20 min).** Without making any decisions, run the randomizer alone for 10 games of your game's typical length, 5 with dice and 5 with the deck. Record productive turns and longest dry spell for each player. Also enter your target numbers and game length in the [dice vs deck interactive](../examples/w06-dice-vs-deck.html) and record its mean and spread for both.
7. **Play both versions (30 min).** With a partner, play one full game with dice and one with the deck. Fill the randomizer log, including each player's one-sentence feeling right after the game.
8. **Decide (home).** Keep dice, keep the deck, or mix them. Update the rules sheet to v0.2 and add a changelog row.
9. **Prepare for lab 07 (home).** Assemble a clean kit: rules sheet, all components in an envelope, and a setup photo so you can reset quickly between testers.

## Deliverable

- The prototype kit, photographed laid out for setup, and the rules sheet v0.2.
- Your notebook pages: question and goal, component list, hole list from the solo test, changelog, randomizer log.
- A decision note, at most 300 words. State the choice (dice, deck or mix). Give the spread you measured for each, meaning the range of productive turns across your 10 runs and the longest dry spell. Tie the choice to your experience goal.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent (4) | Adequate (2-3) | Weak (0-1) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Question and components | Clear question tied to an experience goal; every component mapped to a formal element and justified | Question clear, some components unjustified | No question, or components chosen for looks | 20% |
| Rules sheet | One page, numbered, versioned; a classmate can set up and play one turn without asking anything | One or two ambiguities | Can't be played without the designer | 30% |
| Solo test and changelog | Holes listed and fixed; changelog entries give reasons | Holes listed but some left unfixed | No evidence of solo testing | 15% |
| Dice-or-deck comparison | Both versions run; spread and dry spells reported for each; deck built with correct counts | Both run, measurements incomplete | Only one version, or wrong card counts | 20% |
| Decision note | Choice tied to the goal with a number; states one thing the other option would have given | Choice made, weak link to the goal | No decision or no evidence | 15% |
