# MMO309 · Threat model brief: a costed threat model for one MMO

**Weight:** 30% of the course grade.
**Work mode:** individual, or the same pair you worked with in Lab 01. Pairs add a half-page contribution statement.
**When:** milestone 1 at the end of week 4, milestone 2 at the end of week 9 with a peer review in the week 10 lab, final document at the start of week 15.
**Length:** at most 15 pages plus appendices. Tables count toward the page limit, appendices do not.

## What this component is for

The course's first outcome is "threat-model an MMO", and this is where you show it. Week 1 gave you the method (assets, attackers, entry points, trust boundaries, STRIDE plus the three game families, the loss ledger and the control value `net = r × ALE − B − P`). Every later week gave you a model that turns one row of that ledger into numbers you can defend. The finished document is the one a security lead would hand a producer before a launch. It says which threats to fix first, what each fix costs the studio and its honest players, and which risks the team accepts on purpose.

Lab 01 is your first draft. Labs 04, 06, 08 and 12 each produce a piece you will reuse, and Lab 14 gives you an optional appendix. The [final prototype](final-prototype-brief.md) picks one threat from this document and builds its detector, so choose your game with that in mind.

## Choosing the game

Pick one of these.

- **Driftmark**, the invented MMO from [Lab 01](../labs/lab01.md), extended with at least three features of your own design.
- **A game you are designing**, for example your PR301 world, described in enough detail that a reader can draw its data flows.
- **A shipped MMO**, analyzed from public sources only (developer blogs, patch notes, talks, court records, maintained wikis). Cite every fact with its version or era, as the lectures do.

For a shipped game the rules are strict. You work from what is already public. You do not test, probe, scan, fuzz or automate anything against the live game, its servers, its website or its accounts, including your own account. You do not publish a method for an exploit that is not already public and fixed. If you believe you have found a live vulnerability while reading, stop, report it through the studio's published disclosure channel (week 13), tell the instructor, and leave the details out of your document. A threat model describes what could happen and what it would cost. It never needs a working attack.

## What the document contains

Follow the order from week 1, section 5. Sections 1 to 8 are required. Section 9 is where the later weeks' models go, and every item in it is required unless your game has no such feature, in which case say so in one line.

1. **System summary and data flow diagram.** Processes, data stores, external entities and flows, with every trust boundary drawn as a dashed line. At least eight boundaries for a full MMO. Include support and GM tools, payment, and account recovery, not only the client and server.
2. **Assets**, each with an owner on the team and a note on who notices when it fails (week 1's "loud or silent" column).
3. **Attackers** that apply, with motive, budget and patience.
4. **Threats.** At least 20, generated by running STRIDE on each boundary crossing plus the three game families (automation, design exploits, social attacks). Every STRIDE letter and every family appears at least once. Each threat has `f` and `I` and a one-line source for each guess ("support tickets from Lab 06's log", "assumption").
5. **The loss ledger.** ALE for every threat, sorted, with low and high estimates for at least the five most uncertain rows. Show the stable top five (Lab 01's `stable_top`) and name the one estimate most worth a day of research.
6. **Controls** for at least the top eight threats, each with `r`, `B`, `P` and first-year net value, and a note on year 2 where `B` is mostly one-off. At least two controls carry a nonzero player cost, and at least one lowers the loss of two threats.
7. **Accepted risks**, each with the reason it is accepted.
8. **Review date** and the feature changes that would trigger an early review.
9. **Course models applied.** One short subsection each, with the calculation shown.
   - *Information disclosure (week 2).* The leak per round or per engagement for your main hidden-information asset under your current send policy and one improvement, turned into a change in `I`. If you rely on sampled validation, give `p` and the gold an attacker takes before the first catch.
   - *Movement (week 3).* Your check (rate or credit), the lag tolerance, the largest jump or free speed it gives away, and the spike it forgives. Games with tile or server-timed movement say so and skip the numbers.
   - *Value paths (week 4).* Your value-path inventory, whether every path goes through one transfer primitive with an idempotency key, and the chance of shipping at least one dupe under your assumed per-path risk, before and after.
   - *Client integrity (week 5).* Which cheat classes you defend on the client and which on the server, the privacy and security cost of each client measure, and your ban delay for cheat signatures with the `sessions(W)` calculation behind it.
   - *Bot economics (week 6).* A farm card for your game, the break-even lifetime `A / m`, and the cheapest lever that pushes a bot's expected lifetime below it (your Lab 06 method on your own numbers).
   - *Detection (weeks 7 and 8).* The features you would rely on, ranked by cost to fake, and the precision of your main detector at your estimated prevalence, before and after a successful ban wave.
   - *Accounts (week 9).* The five-step takeover chain for your game with one defense per step, a trade hold justified with `r(H) = 1 − e^(−H/m)` and your `m`, and its daily delay cost.
   - *RMT (week 10).* Where your trade graph would find hubs, the expansion rule you would use, and how theft victims are kept out of the ban queue.
   - *Incident response (week 11).* For your top dupe or exploit threat, your estimates of `P`, `b`, `w` and `λ`, the detection delay at which tracing becomes cheaper than rolling back, and the runbook rule that follows (rollback, trace and delete with refunds from the ring, or a trade freeze while you decide). Then how you would sanction the players involved by tier, sized so the ring's net is negative, and the safeguards you would run before any batched ban.
   - *Sanctions (week 12).* A sanction ladder with a written `C` for each rung, and the evidence each rung needs (your Lab 12 policy, adapted). State what data you keep and for how long.
   - *Disclosure (week 13).* Your two-track disclosure policy (security vulnerabilities and gameplay exploits), the `B*` for your highest-value exploit class at your detection time, and what you publish after a fix.
   - *Case lesson (week 14).* One case from the week 14 lecture written in the seven-row case template, your top bot or cheat threat scored on the response scorecard (uptime and saving for at least three levers, with `live / down` naming the term to move), and the one transferable rule you apply to your game.

Appendices may hold your full threat table, the ledger script and its output, Lab 12's policy table, and Lab 14's response scorecard if you want to include it.

## Milestone 1: scope and diagram (end of week 4, 5 points)

Two pages at most. The game you chose and why, the data flow diagram with trust boundaries, the asset list with owners, and the attacker list. Feedback comes back in the week 5 lab. If you chose a shipped game, list the public sources you will use.

## Milestone 2: ledger and controls (end of week 9, 10 points)

The threat table (at least 15 threats so far), the ledger with low and high estimates, and at least five costed controls. In the week 10 lab you swap with another student or pair, and each writes a one-page review. The review names the one number in the other ledger most likely to be wrong, says which way, and names one threat the other document is missing. Your milestone 2 mark covers both your ledger and the review you wrote.

## Final document (start of week 15, 85 points)

Submit one PDF and a folder with your ledger script (Lab 01's tool is fine), its input CSV files and any other script whose numbers appear in the document. Every number in the document must come from a formula shown in the text or from a script in the folder. A marker will run the scripts with `.venv/bin/python`.

In the week 15 session, while you demo the [final prototype](final-prototype-brief.md), expect two questions about this document in the style of the [exam bank](exam-bank.md), rewritten with your own numbers.

## Rubric

Each criterion is marked in bands. Excellent earns the full points, Adequate earns half to three quarters, and Weak earns a quarter or less.

| Criterion | Points | Excellent | Adequate | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Milestone 1 | 5 | On time; diagram with eight or more boundaries including support, payment and recovery; assets have owners | On time, diagram thin or boundaries missing | Late or missing |
| Milestone 2 and peer review | 10 | Ledger and five controls on time; review names a specific wrong number with its direction and a missing threat | Ledger on time, review generic | Late, or no review |
| System, assets and attackers | 10 | Final diagram complete and consistent with the threats; every asset has an owner and a failure mode; attackers have motive and budget | Complete, a few gaps between diagram and threats | Diagram or assets missing |
| Threat enumeration | 10 | 20 or more threats; every STRIDE letter and all three game families covered; every `f` and `I` has a source | 20 threats with a category missing, or sources thin | Fewer than 15 threats |
| Loss ledger and uncertainty | 15 | Correct ALEs; low and high estimates on the uncertain rows; stable top five shown; the research-worthy estimate named and justified | Ledger correct, uncertainty handled for only a few rows | Ledger with arithmetic errors or no ranking |
| Controls | 15 | Eight or more controls with `r`, `B`, `P` and net value; year 2 considered; player cost argued, not assumed zero; one control covers two threats | Controls costed but `P` treated lightly or year 2 missing | Controls without numbers |
| Course models applied | 20 | Every applicable subsection of section 9 present, each with a correct calculation on the game's own numbers and a decision that follows from it | Most subsections present, some calculations copied from lectures rather than applied | Fewer than half present |
| Accepted risks, review and response | 5 | Accepted risks with reasons; review date and triggers; incident response plan consistent with the sanction ladder | Present but brief | Missing |
| Evidence and writing | 10 | Every game fact cited with version or era; assumptions marked as assumptions; scripts reproduce every number; plain and within the page limit | Minor citation gaps or length overrun | Uncited facts, numbers that do not reproduce, or unsafe content |
| **Total** | **100** | | | |

A document that includes a working exploit method for a live, unfixed issue, or evidence of testing against a live service, is capped at the Weak band for "Evidence and writing" and referred to the instructor.

## Rules and advice

- Start from your Lab 01 output. The marker expects the ledger to grow, not to be rewritten from scratch.
- Put your numbers where a reader can challenge them. "Every symbol is a guess" (week 1), and a written guess can be argued with.
- You may use documentation, forums and AI assistants to understand a concept or check arithmetic, and you must say so in one line in an appendix. The estimates, the ranking and the decisions must be yours, and you must be able to explain each in the viva.
- Late work follows the course policy in the [labs brief](labs-brief.md). Milestones submitted late still get feedback, but earn at most half their points.
