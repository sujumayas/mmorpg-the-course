CS102 Programming for Games II · Week 11 · Worked example

Eight frames to keep the chain

A teaching model of Super Mario 64's jump chain, using the numbers in the community decompilation: a 4-frame landing, a countdown of 5, and a triple jump that needs forward speed above 20. The same small hop can come from two different guards. This page shows which one fired.

A. When: frames 0 to 120

Press 1 is on frame 0. Press 2 comes d2 frames after the first touchdown, press 3 comes d3 frames after the second. Airtimes (20, 24, 30 frames) are invented.

Window: what press 2 gives at each delay d2 (everything else fixed)

B. Why: the machine and the guard that decided each press

The node Mario is in at the playhead has a thick outline. When the playhead crosses a transition, that edge lights up and the caption names its guard.

Model: the lecture's Mario class, ported line for line. Each frame the countdown drops by 1 if above 0, then action handlers run until one does not switch action (at most 4 passes; 1 with the rerun toggle off). Landing frames set the countdown to 5 (0 after a triple jump). Numbers from the n64decomp Super Mario 64 decompilation; the model leaves out steep floors, quicksand, held objects, the Wing Cap and wall kicks. No game art is shown.
