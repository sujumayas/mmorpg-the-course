The scan grows with the realm. The index barely moves.

A thousand times more rows means a thousand times more pages to scan, and one or two more pages through a B-tree. The catch is on the right: the index only wins for selective queries.

Slider snaps to 1, 1.2, 2 and 5 × powers of 10. Type any value in the box.

1.0 = everything cached (SSD/RAM). PostgreSQL’s default is 4.0.

Model from DB201 week 4: h = ⌈logf N⌉, scan = ⌈N/R⌉, index = h + k (scattered) or h + ⌈k/R⌉ (clustered); cost of an index read at selectivity s is h + s·N·cr. The planner cost is a simplified version of PostgreSQL’s, which also counts CPU per row. Shapes are generic; no game art.
