# MA101 midterm and final brief

**Weights:** midterm 15%, final 25% of the course grade.
**Question source:** the [exam bank](exam-bank.md), with numbers changed.
**Related:** [problem sets](problem-sets-brief.md), [curve-design project](curve-design-project-brief.md).

## Format

| | Midterm | Final |
|---|---|---|
| When | week 8, in the second class session | week 15, in the exam period |
| Length | 90 minutes | 150 minutes |
| Scope | weeks 1 to 7 | weeks 1 to 14 |
| Total | 100 marks | 100 marks |
| Part A | 6 short compute questions, 5 marks each (30) | 8 short compute questions, 4 marks each (32) |
| Part B | 3 multi-part compute questions, 15 marks each (45) | 4 multi-part questions, 12 marks each (48), at least one cross-week |
| Part C | 1 explain, critique or design question (25) | 1 explain, critique or design question (20) |
| Allowed | non-programmable scientific calculator; one A4 sheet of your own notes, both sides, handwritten | the same calculator; two A4 sheets, both sides, handwritten |
| Not allowed | phones, laptops, graphing or programmable calculators, printed notes | the same |

Every fact about a shipped game that a question needs is printed in the question, as it is in the bank. You never need to remember a game's constants. You do need to remember the models: the forms of the functions, how to invert them, and what their asymptotes, breakpoints and slopes mean.

## How questions are drawn from the bank

The bank is public from week 1. Studying it is the intended way to prepare. The exams use it as follows.

- **Eligibility.** The midterm draws only questions tagged `midterm, final`. The final draws from the whole bank.
- **Coverage.** Every week in scope is tested at least once, counting cross-week questions for each week they list. On the final, weeks 8 to 14 carry 50 to 60% of the marks, weeks 1 to 7 carry 25 to 35%, and cross-week questions carry at least 12%.
- **New numbers.** Every compute question is used with new numbers. The model, the steps and the structure stay the same. The instructor recomputes each new answer in a script and keeps it with the exam key.
- **Open questions.** Explain, critique and design questions may be used as written or with a new scenario that needs the same model.
- **Type mix.** At least 60% of the marks come from compute questions. Part C is always an explain, critique or design question.
- **No repeats.** A question used on the midterm does not reappear on the final in any form.
- **Two versions.** Each exam is set in two versions with different numbers. The second is used for make-up sittings.

## Marking compute questions

- **Method and answer.** A compute question's marks are split 40% for setting up the right model with the right values, 40% for carrying it out and 20% for the final answer with units and a sense check.
- **Follow-through.** An error is penalised once. Later steps that correctly use a wrong earlier value keep their method marks.
- **Tolerance.** Answers within one unit in the third significant figure are accepted. Where the model itself rounds (a `floor` in a stat formula, whole purchases, whole levels), the rounded value is required.
- **Discriminant and domain answers.** "It cannot reach" or "not in the domain" is a full answer when the working shows why.

## Rubric for open questions

Part C questions (and the explain, critique or design parts of Part B) are marked with this rubric. The points shown are for the midterm's 25-mark Part C. For the final's 20-mark Part C, multiply each row by 0.8.

| Criterion | Points | Full | Partial | Minimal | None |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model | 6 | 6. Names the right model from the lectures and states it correctly | 4. Right model, stated with a small error | 2. Related model, or right model misapplied | 0 |
| Numbers as evidence | 8 | 8. At least two relevant numbers computed correctly and used in the argument | 5. One correct number, or two with a slip | 2. Numbers present but not relevant or mostly wrong | 0 |
| Reasoning | 6 | 6. Connects the numbers to what the player sees, feels or does | 4. Connection made but loose | 2. Restates numbers without interpretation | 0 |
| Recommendation and trade-off | 5 | 5. A concrete change or choice, with its cost stated | 3. Concrete change, cost missing | 1. Vague direction ("balance it better") | 0 |

A design or critique answer that reaches a different conclusion from the bank's model answer earns full marks when it meets every row. The bank answers show one good argument, not the only one.

## Missed exams and adjustments

- A student who misses an exam for a documented reason sits the second version within two weeks.
- Adjustments such as extra time follow the university's disability and accessibility office. Tell the instructor at least two weeks before the exam.
- Graded scripts and a worked key are returned within two weeks. The key shows the full working for the numbers actually used.
