# MMO305 · Reading responses brief

**Weight:** 20% of the course grade.
**Covers:** six written responses to the course readings, and the model labs 01, 02, 03, 07, 08, 09 and 11.
**Related briefs:** [field-observation-brief.md](field-observation-brief.md) (labs 04, 10, 12 and 13 count there) and [final-design-proposal-brief.md](final-design-proposal-brief.md) (labs 05, 06 and 14 count there). Practice questions for every week are in the [exam bank](exam-bank.md).

## What this component is for

The course outcomes behind this component are "explain how game systems create or erode social ties" and "diagnose and mitigate griefing and toxicity." Both depend on reading research carefully. That means knowing what a study measured, in which game and which era, and what it cannot tell you. It also means being able to take a finding out of its paper and use it on a case the authors never saw.

So every response asks the same three things. What does the reading claim, in your words? Can you apply the week's model to a new case, with numbers? And what is the study's main limit, and which way does it bias the result? The labs in Part B practise the same move with data instead of prose.

### What a response contains

Each response is 500 to 700 words plus any table or calculation. Use these four headings, in this order.

1. **Claim.** The reading's central claim in at most 120 words of your own. Name the game or community studied, the era, and the method (survey, census, interviews, participant observation, formal model).
2. **Apply.** Take the model from the matching lecture and use it on a case the reading did not study. It can be a game you play, a feature you would design, or an invented scenario. Show at least one number you computed with the model, with the inputs stated. For window 2, for example, you might build a six-snapshot census of a group you know and compute its three densities. For window 6, you might estimate `p`, `r_h` and `r_o` for a game you play and say what share of incidents punishment could ever reach.
3. **Limit.** One limit of the study that matters for a designer. Say which way it biases the finding, not only that it exists. Useful angles are the era and patch (a 2005 census is not current WoW), the sample (who could the method reach, as in week 13's length bias), and the measure (does the study count co-presence and call it social play?).
4. **Question.** One question you would put to the authors or bring to class, in one sentence.

### Rules for every response

- Paraphrase. Quote at most one short phrase from the reading, in quotation marks with a page number. Long quotation scores zero on the Claim criterion.
- Cite the reading and any other source in a short reference list. A game fact that is not in the lectures needs its own source with the version or era.
- Numbers must be reproducible. State every input. A calculation that cannot be checked is marked as if it were missing.
- Write it yourself. Discussing readings with classmates is encouraged. The words and the case you apply the model to must be yours.

### Response rubric (10 points each)

| Criterion | Points | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak or missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claim | 3 | 3: accurate, in your own words, names game, era and method | 2.5: accurate, one of game, era or method missing | 1.5-2: broadly right but vague or partly wrong | 0-1: wrong, missing, or mostly quoted |
| Apply | 4 | 4: model used on a genuinely new case, inputs stated, number correct, a design consequence drawn from it | 3: new case and correct number, consequence thin | 2: model named and applied loosely, or number has an error | 0-1: no model, or restates the lecture's own example |
| Limit | 2 | 2: a limit that matters for design, with the direction of bias | 1.5: real limit, direction not stated | 1: generic limit ("small sample") | 0: missing |
| Question and writing | 1 | 1: a sharp question; plain, well-organized prose within length | 0.5: question generic or writing hard to follow | — | 0: missing |

Part A is the mean of your six responses, scaled to 100. A missing required response scores 0 and cannot be replaced by an optional window.

### How Part B is computed

1. Each lab is marked out of 100 with the rubric printed in it. These seven labs use four bands with percentage weights, where Excellent is 4, Adequate is 2 or 3 and Missing or wrong is 0 or 1. Each criterion scores `weight × band / 4`. For example, an Adequate (3) on a 20% criterion scores `20 × 3/4 = 15`. The marker records the band chosen.
2. Your lowest lab score is dropped.
3. The six remaining labs are averaged with equal weight. The average is Part B.

A missing lab scores 0 and is a candidate for dropping like any other score.

## How the component grade is computed

`component = 0.5 × Part A + 0.5 × Part B`, out of 100. It is worth 20% of the course.

## Late work and extensions

- A response or lab submitted up to 48 hours late loses 10 points out of 100 (1 point on a 10-point response). After that it scores 0. A late optional response still counts as your choice for that window.
- You have two free 48-hour extensions over the semester, usable on any response or on any lab in this brief. Tell the course team before the deadline.
- Longer extensions need documented illness or emergency.

## Academic integrity

Discuss the readings and the labs with classmates. The words, calculations and cases you submit must be your own. If you reuse a script or a sheet from a classmate or a public source, name it and say what you changed. Paraphrase every source and quote at most one short phrase from each.
