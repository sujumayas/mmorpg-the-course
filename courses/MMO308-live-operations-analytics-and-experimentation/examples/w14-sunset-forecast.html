Two forecasts that agree for a year, then part by eighteen months

A single exponential and a core-plus-casual mixture both fit the first year of MAU. On a linear axis they look alike. On a log axis only the mixture bends, and the sunset dates they give sit far apart, right where six months' notice forces you to choose.

Mixture: M(t) = M0 × (w × 2^(−t/hc) + (1 − w) × 2^(−t/hk)). Single rate through months 0 and f: λ = ln(M0 / M(f)) / f, S(t) = M0 × e^(−λt). Crossing is the first month on a 0.1-month grid where a × MAU ≤ C. The six-month ratio is MAU(t) / MAU(t − 6). Error costs sum over 0.1-month steps from the single crossing to the mixture crossing. Profit forgone is the mixture's profit you give up by closing early. Loss taken is what running on costs if the single rate is true.
