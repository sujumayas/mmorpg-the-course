# MMO301 · World design bible brief

**Weight:** 40% of the course grade.
**Part A:** six labs that each draft one chapter, [06](../labs/lab06.md), [07](../labs/lab07.md), [10](../labs/lab10.md), [11](../labs/lab11.md), [12](../labs/lab12.md) and [14](../labs/lab14.md). 12% of the course.
**Part B:** the bible itself, specified below. 24% of the course. Due at the start of week 15.
**Part C:** a 15-minute presentation and viva in week 15. 4% of the course.
**Also reused, not graded again:** your death rule from [Lab 05](../labs/lab05.md) and your sunset plan from [Lab 13](../labs/lab13.md). Both are marked in the [case studies](case-studies-brief.md). Here they are revised into chapters.

## What this component is for

Every lecture in this course ended with a design decision the historical case forced on its designers. How many people share a place. What a death costs. Whether a boss is rival. What lasts and for how long. How the world ends. The bible asks you to make all of those decisions for one world of your own, with numbers, and to show that they agree with each other.

A bible that reads well but contradicts itself fails. If chapter 4 plans three map copies at peak so that players see strangers, and chapter 6 says players find each other by recognizing faces in the field, the two chapters are fighting. The viva will find it. The most heavily weighted line in the rubric is consistency.

## Part B: the bible (60 points)

### Your world

One persistent, shared online world of your own design. It must be a virtual world by Bartle's definition from [week 1](../lectures/w01-what-is-a-virtual-world-bartles-definition.md), and chapter 1 must say which property settings you chose. It may be any genre and any platform. It may not be a copy of a shipped game's setting, characters or art, and it must include no copyrighted material. If it borrows a structure from a shipped game, name the game, cite the lecture that covers it, and say what you changed.

### Chapters

6,000 to 8,000 words across all chapters, plus the spreadsheets, charts and one-page concept. Use these chapters in this order.

1. **Concept** (from Lab 14). The one-page concept, with the sentence, three to five pillars and the seven numbered commitments, revised after the peer critique, with the change log in an appendix.
2. **Who it is for** (weeks [8](../lectures/w08-player-types-bartle-1996-and-the-heros-journey.md) and [9](../lectures/w09-motivation-research-beyond-bartle-yee.md)). Target players described by component scores, not types. Score your five most important features on Yee's three components and show that each component you claim to serve has features serving it. Use your Bartle model runs to choose a `protection` level for open conflict and say which of Bartle's configurations you are aiming for. Reuse what you learned in the [player-type critique](player-type-critique-brief.md). Do not reuse its prose.
3. **Structure** (week [7](../lectures/w07-themepark-vs-sandbox.md), Lab 07). Themepark-first, sandbox-first or hybrid, argued with `s_T`, `s_S`, `s_H` and `N*` for a light and a heavy player. State the population drop that would break your choice.
4. **Density and shards** (weeks [1](../lectures/w01-what-is-a-virtual-world-bartles-definition.md), [13](../lectures/w13-worlds-that-died-shutdowns-and-their-lessons.md) and [14](../lectures/w14-designing-a-world-concept.md), Labs 01 and 14). The density budget with your daily curve, the field area, `E` at peak and trough, and your copies or pooling policy. The shard count at launch and a merge trigger written as a rule with a number. Say how two players who met yesterday find each other today.
5. **Risk and conduct** (weeks [5](../lectures/w05-world-of-warcraft-accessibility-and-the-themepark.md), [6](../lectures/w06-eve-online-the-single-shard-sandbox.md) and [11](../lectures/w11-kosters-laws-of-online-world-design.md), Labs 05, 06 and 11). Your death rule in `(t, x)` form by level band with `λ*` for three player profiles. Your risk gradient as a zone table with the price of aggression in each zone and the new-player test. A laws review of your single riskiest feature with exposure numbers, isolation rules, and the telemetry that would catch what remains.
6. **Identity** (week [10](../lectures/w10-immersion-identity-and-the-avatar.md), Lab 10). Character creation categories with expected option shares, `M` against `M_eff`, the crowd size for even odds of a twin in your busiest hub, and your name policy. Say which rung of Bartle's immersion ladder your world is built to reach and which features carry players there.
7. **Persistence and time** (week [12](../lectures/w12-persistence-and-time-what-should-last.md), Lab 12). The persistence budget, at least ten object classes, with `λ`, `W`, the clock-reset rule, `S = λW` and per-zone counts. Your world clock and reset schedule. What you promise players will last.
8. **Economy** (week [3](../lectures/w03-ultima-online-the-simulation-dream-and-its-limits.md), Lab 03). Closed loop or faucet and drain, with the steady supply or steady stored stock computed. The drains that ship at launch and who pays for each. One diagram. If you are taking MMO304, you may build on its [week 14 lecture](../../MMO304-virtual-economies-i-faucets-sinks-and-markets/lectures/w14-designing-an-economy-from-scratch.md), but this chapter is marked on the week 3 model.
9. **Ending** (week 13, Lab 13). The five-question sunset plan for your own world, covering the trigger (with a number), notice period, final content, what players keep and preservation. Check that it can be run with a team of three and three months' notice.

**Appendices** (not counted in the word limit) hold the spreadsheets or scripts for chapters 3 to 8, each with its lecture check values passing; the Lab 14 peer critique and change log; a consistency table (below); and a source list.

### The consistency table

One table, one row per pair of chapters that constrain each other. For each row, name the shared number or rule and show that both chapters use the same value. At minimum include these rows.

| Chapters | What must agree |
|---|---|
| 1 and all | Every commitment's number appears unchanged in the chapter that develops it |
| 2 and 5 | The `protection` level in chapter 2 and the zone prices in chapter 5 |
| 3 and 4 | `N` in reach in chapter 3 and players per shard or copy in chapter 4 |
| 4 and 6 | The crowd size in your busiest hub |
| 4 and 7 | Zone count and the per-zone object counts |
| 7 and 8 | Lifetimes of items that are also economic drains |
| 4 and 9 | The merge trigger and the sunset trigger, which should not fire in the wrong order |

### Rules for facts and numbers

- Every claim about a real game needs a source with its version, patch or era. The lectures' Sources sections count for the facts they list.
- Label every invented number. A bible is made of design decisions, and the reader must be able to tell a decision from a claim about the world.
- The one-page concept must fit on one A4 page at 11-point text.

## Part C: presentation and viva (10 points)

In week 15 you present your world for 5 minutes (at most six slides, one of them the density chart) and answer questions for 10. Examiners bring two or three questions from the [exam bank](exam-bank.md), rewritten with numbers from your bible, and at least one question about a row of your consistency table. You may bring your spreadsheets. The viva also serves as the spot check for case study 3.

| Band | Points | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Excellent | 9-10 | Recomputes a number from the bible on request; traces a design decision to a model and a historical case; answers a "what if" (population halves, a loophole is found) with a number |
| Good | 7-8 | Explains every number asked about; one "what if" answered in words only |
| Adequate | 5-6 | Explains most numbers; unsure where one came from |
| Weak | 0-4 | Cannot explain the bible's central numbers |

## Late work

The bible may be submitted up to 48 hours late for a loss of 6 of its 60 points. After that it is marked for feedback only and scores 0 on Part B. The presentation cannot be moved except for documented illness or emergency. The chapter labs follow the lab late rule in the [case studies brief](case-studies-brief.md#late-work-and-integrity).
