# GS101 · Lab 12: A procedural-rhetoric close reading

**Week:** 12 · **Time:** 2 hours in lab plus about 3 hours at home · **Weight:** draft of your second close reading (the two close readings are 40% of the course)

## Objective

Write a close reading of an indie game that argues through its rules. You will find the game's budget, compute it from sourced numbers, price its moral choice, and turn the numbers into a claim. This lab applies the six "find the budget" steps from [week 12, section 4](../lectures/w12-indie-games-and-the-auteur-designer.md) inside the five-step close-reading method from [week 1](../lectures/w01-play-before-computers.md).

## Starter materials

- A spreadsheet and a word processor.
- One game from this list, or another with the instructor's approval. Each must be a game you can play for at least two hours.
  - *Papers, Please* (Lucas Pope, 2013). Use it only if you choose a different resource than the lecture did (for example, time within a day, or the tokens and bribes of the later story), or if you study a later part of the game with a changed rule set.
  - *Cart Life* (Richard Hofmeier, 2010). Free from the author; resource is money and hours.
  - *This War of Mine* (11 bit studios, 2014). Resource is food, medicine and materials across day and night.
  - *Stardew Valley* (Eric Barone, 2016), or another game that argues through a budget, with approval.
- The budget template (copy it into your sheet):

| Step | Question | Your answer | Source (wiki page, in-game screen, your play log with timestamp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scarce resource(s) | | |
| 2 | Income rule, and what earning more costs | | |
| 3 | Needs per cycle, mandatory vs optional | | |
| 4 | Margin for a careful player (computed) | | |
| 5 | Price of the moral choice, and whether it changes | | |
| 6 | The claim the numbers make | | |

- A filled example row for reference, from the lecture: step 4 for *Papers, Please*, rent 25, heat 10, food 5 × 4 relatives, 5 credits per correct entrant, so a careful inspector needs 11 correct entrants a day and falls 5 credits short at 10.
- A play-log template: day or cycle number, income, each expense, choices made, one line on how the choice felt.

## Steps

1. **Play and log (in lab, 60 min).** Play at least six in-game cycles (days, shifts, nights). Fill the play log as you go. Screenshot each end-of-cycle screen for your own reference; do not include game art in the submission.
2. **Verify the rules (20 min).** For every number you use, find a source: the game's own screens, a maintained wiki page (name it and the game version), or a developer statement. Mark anything you inferred from your play log as "observed".
3. **Fill the budget template (20 min).** Compute the margin in step 4 with a small table of at least four rows, as the lecture does. Show one row's arithmetic in full.
4. **Price the choice (20 min, at home).** Find one kind or principled action and compute its cost in the game's resource at two different moments. Does the price change? Why?
5. **Context (at home).** Who made the game, how was it funded and published, and what did its maker say about it? Use this to explain the budget, not to replace it. Name at least one person besides the credited "author" who shaped the game.
6. **Write (at home).** Draft the close reading described below.

## Deliverable

A close reading of about 900 words, plus the filled template and play log as an appendix. The essay must:

- open with a concrete moment from your play log;
- include one small table of computed numbers from the game's rules;
- state the price of the moral choice and whether it changes;
- make one claim a reader could dispute, using the form "In this game's world, X costs Y, so Z";
- cite every rule and number, with version or era.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent (4) | Adequate (2-3) | Missing or wrong (0-1) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Budget computed correctly | Table recomputes; one row's arithmetic shown; sources for each number | Minor errors or one unsourced number | Numbers wrong or unsourced | 25% |
| Price of the moral choice | Computed at two moments, change (or lack of change) explained | Computed once | Asserted without numbers | 15% |
| Claim | Specific, arguable, and follows from the numbers | Arguable but loosely tied to the numbers | Description only | 25% |
| Context and authorship | Context explains the budget; at least one collaborator named; auteur claims handled critically | Context present but decorative | Missing | 15% |
| Evidence and writing | Opens with a logged moment, paraphrases sources, no long quotes, clear prose | Some vague or unsupported sentences | Hard to follow | 20% |
