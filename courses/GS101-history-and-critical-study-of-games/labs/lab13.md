# GS101 · Lab 13: The rules-fiction ledger

**Week:** 13 · **Time:** 2 hours in the lab plus up to 3 hours at home · **Weight:** practice for the second close reading (formative, feedback only; you may develop it into the graded piece)

**Lecture:** [Week 13, ludology vs narratology](../lectures/w13-ludology-vs-narratology.md)

## Objective

Play one game with your own hands, record every choice its fiction takes a position on, and build a full rules-fiction ledger with sourced numbers. Then write a close reading that uses the six-step method, including the new "check the layers" step. By the end you will have tested a critical claim against a game's rules instead of repeating it.

## Starter materials

Everything below is described here. There is nothing to download except the game.

- **A game from the approved list.** Pick one you can play for 45 minutes in the lab. Any version is fine, but you must record which one (platform, release or patch). Approved: BioShock (2007 or the Remastered edition), Papers, Please (2013), Undertale (2015), or any other game with a repeated choice the fiction cares about, approved by your tutor at the start of the lab. Good candidates have a moral choice, a mercy/kill option, a theft or honesty option, or a resource that the story says is harmful to collect.
- **A published reading to test.** Pick one: Hocking (2007) on BioShock, a professional review of your game that makes a claim about its story and systems, or a claim from a forum or video essay that your tutor approves. You need one sentence from it that says what the game's rules and fiction are doing.
- **The choice log** (copy this table; one row per time the choice comes up).

| # | Time in session | Choice situation (what the fiction shows) | Option taken | Rules result (resource and amount) | Fiction framing of the option (+1, 0, −1) | Evidence for the framing (dialogue, visual, music, UI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **The ledger** (copy this table; one row per option).

## Steps

1. **Choose and record (10 min).** Choose your game and your published reading. Write down the version you are playing and the one-sentence claim you will test.
2. **Play and log (45 min).** Play from the start or from a save your tutor approves. Every time the choice comes up, fill in a row of the choice log before you move on. Take the option you would naturally take. Note the exact reward the game shows you. If the game hides the number, write what you can observe (a bar filling, an item gained).
3. **Find the whole-game numbers (25 min).** Use a maintained wiki or official source to find how many times the choice occurs in a full playthrough, and any delayed payoffs (gifts, unlocks, endings, reputation). Write the source and the game version for each number. If sources disagree, record both and say which you trust and why.
4. **Build the ledger (15 min).** Fill in one row per option. Compute `I_encounter` and `I_game`. If a payoff is not a number (an ending, a unique item), write it in the "other payoffs" column and say in words how it changes the balance.
5. **Classify and test (15 min).** Classify the relationship. Then return to your published reading. Does the ledger support it, complicate it, or contradict it? Does the claim hold for a first-time player, a returning player, or both?
6. **Write (at home).** Write the close reading described below.

## Deliverable

One document with:
- The choice log from your session (at least 4 rows; if your game offers fewer choices in 45 minutes, add rows from a second session).
- The completed ledger with every number sourced and versioned.
- The classification sheet.
- An 800-word close reading that follows the six-step method (describe, find the machine, name the experience, check the layers, place it in context, make one claim). The claim must be arguable and must rest on the ledger. One paragraph must quote the one-sentence claim from your published reading (quotation marks, citation) and test it against your numbers.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent (4) | Adequate (2-3) | Missing or wrong (0-1) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Choice log | Four or more rows, each with the shown reward and specific evidence for the framing | Rows present but framing evidence vague ("it felt bad") | Fewer than three rows, or no evidence | 15% |
| Ledger and sources | Every number sourced with version; delayed payoffs included; disagreements between sources handled | Most numbers sourced; one delayed payoff missed | Numbers unsourced or per-encounter only | 25% |
| Ratios and classification | Both ratios correct; classification justified; non-numeric payoffs discussed | Ratios correct, classification asserted without reasons | Ratios wrong or missing | 15% |
| Test of the published reading | States exactly what the ledger confirms or complicates, and for which kind of player | Engages the reading but only agrees or disagrees | Reading not tested | 20% |
| Claim and method | One arguable claim resting on the ledger; all six steps visible | Claim present but loosely tied to the numbers | No claim, or fiction-only reading | 15% |
| Writing | 750-850 words, plain and precise, sources cited | Within 20% of length, some unclear sentences | Far off length or hard to follow | 10% |
