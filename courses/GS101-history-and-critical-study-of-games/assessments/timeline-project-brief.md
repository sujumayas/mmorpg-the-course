# GS101 · Timeline project brief

**Weight:** 25% of the course grade, marked out of 100.
**Work mode:** individual.
**Due:** final submission at the end of week 13. Three checkpoints before that (see Milestones).
**Course outcome it assesses:** place major games and genres on a historical timeline with causes.

## The task

Build a sourced, dated timeline of games from pre-digital play to live-service games, and explain why things happened when they did. The timeline is not a list of famous releases. Every entry is a claim with a date, a source and a type of evidence, and at least a third of the entries name a cause.

Most of the raw material comes from labs you will already have done. The project asks you to check it, join it into one timeline and argue across it.

| Strand | Eras | Labs that feed it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Play, computers and coins | Ancient games to the arcade golden age | [Lab 01](../labs/lab01.md), [Lab 02](../labs/lab02.md), [Lab 03](../labs/lab03.md) |
| 2. Consoles and the crash | 1972 to 1986 | [Lab 04](../labs/lab04.md) |
| 3. Lineages and the PC | 1971 to 2017 | [Lab 06](../labs/lab06.md), [Lab 07](../labs/lab07.md) |
| 4. Online worlds and live service | 1978 to the present | [Lab 09](../labs/lab09.md) (with its week 10 World of Warcraft extension), [Lab 11](../labs/lab11.md) |

## What you submit

### 1. The timeline dataset (spreadsheet)

At least 40 entries across the four strands, with at least 6 in each strand and at least one entry before 1950 and one after 2015. One row per entry, with these columns:

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Date | As precise as the source allows |
| Precision | Day, month, year, decade or disputed |
| Event | One sentence, naming the game, company or person |
| Strand | 1 to 4 |
| Evidence type | Artifact, image or text, reconstruction, company figure, contemporary press, later account, or model estimate (week 1 and week 9 vocabulary) |
| Cause type | Technology, business, culture, or none (a plain event) |
| Mechanism | For cause entries, one sentence naming the mechanism, using a course model variable where one fits (for example `1 / L` for the coin-op, `S` against `D` for the shelf, `L_S` for mod ownership, `W` for world size, `p` for free-to-play) |
| Alternative | For cause entries, the strongest rival explanation in one sentence |
| Source | Title, publisher or site, date, and the version or era the fact belongs to |

At least 14 entries must be cause entries, with at least 3 of each cause type (technology, business, culture). A cause entry that names a mechanism but no alternative explanation counts as half an entry. Question 60 in the [exam bank](exam-bank.md) shows a model entry.

### 2. Two figures

- **A calendar timeline** of all 40 or more entries, strands on separate rows, with marker shape showing the evidence type.
- **One re-aligned or re-scaled view** that shows a pattern the calendar hides. Choose one: mod chains aligned at host release (week 7), MMO populations on a log scale with `S` and `W` in linked panels (weeks 9 and 10), or a crash-era revenue chart with your shelf-model scenarios (week 4). Label the scale and say in the caption what the view shows that the calendar does not.

Any tool is fine, including a spreadsheet or hand drawing photographed clearly. No game logos, box art or screenshots.

### 3. The causal commentary (1,200 to 1,500 words)

Pick one chain of causes that runs across at least three eras of the course and argue it. Examples of the kind of question that works:

- "Who controls supply, and what did each answer cost?" (the 2600, Nintendo's licensing, Steam Direct, an MMO drop rate)
- "How has the way players pay changed how long games want you to play?" (coin-op, box, hourly, subscription, free-to-play)
- "How did the ability to copy and modify software create genres?" (PDP-1 sharing, Doom's WAD files, the Warcraft III editor)

The commentary must:

- state a claim in the first paragraph that a reader could dispute;
- use at least two course models with numbers, each applied to at least one of your entries;
- separate what the evidence shows from what a model or reconstruction adds (week 1's rule);
- give the strongest alternative explanation for your chain and say why you prefer yours;
- cite entries by their row number.

### 4. Contested-claims appendix

At least three entries where sources disagree or a popular story is wrong. For each, give both versions, both sources and your judgment. The course has already shown you several (the Akalabeth 1979 or 1980 date, the E.T. return figures, "the first video game", the reach of the 1983 crash). At least one of your three must be a case you found yourself.

## Rubric for the final submission (70 points)

| Criterion | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Coverage and accuracy** (15) | 14-15: 40+ entries across all four strands and both ends of the range; dates and precision correct; eras and versions stated | 11-13: Requirements met; two or three small date or precision errors | 8-10: Short of one requirement (entries, strand balance or range), or several errors | 0-7: Well short of 40 entries, or many wrong dates |
| **Causes and mechanisms** (15) | 14-15: 14+ cause entries, 3+ of each type; each names a mechanism with a model variable where one fits and a real alternative | 11-13: Counts met; some mechanisms vague or alternatives weak | 8-10: Too few cause entries, or causes stated as single labels ("greed", "bad games") | 0-7: Causes missing or asserted without mechanism |
| **Sources and evidence types** (15) | 14-15: Every entry sourced with version or era; evidence types correct; company figures, press and estimates kept apart; wiki citations followed where the labs required it | 11-13: One or two unsourced entries or misclassified evidence types | 8-10: Several unsourced entries, or estimates presented as facts | 0-7: Sources missing or invented |
| **Figures** (10) | 9-10: Calendar readable with evidence type by shape; second view reveals a pattern and the caption says what | 7-8: Both present; caption or labelling thin | 5-6: One figure weak or misleading (unlabelled log scale, mixed metrics on one axis) | 0-4: Missing or unreadable |
| **Causal commentary** (10) | 9-10: Disputable claim; three eras; two models with numbers; evidence and model kept apart; alternative taken seriously | 7-8: Claim and models present; one era or the alternative thin | 5-6: Narrative with little argument, or one model only | 0-4: A summary of the timeline |
| **Contested claims** (5) | 5: Three cases, both versions sourced, a reasoned judgment, one found independently | 4: Three cases, judgments brief | 3: Two cases, or all three from lectures only | 0-2: Missing or one-sided |

Total with milestones: 10 (M1) + 10 (M2) + 10 (M3) + 70 (final) = 100.

## Rules

- **Facts need versions and eras.** "Ultima Online had 250,000 subscribers" is not an entry. "Ultima Online reached about 250,000 subscribers by October 2000 (Wikipedia, "Ultima Online", accessed September 2026)" is.
- **Estimates are allowed, invented facts are not.** A model estimate goes in the dataset with the evidence type "model estimate" and its assumptions in the mechanism column.
- **Your own words.** Paraphrase and cite. Quote at most one short phrase from any one source.
- **No copyrighted images.** Draw everything generically.
- **AI and writing tools** follow the program's current policy. Whatever tools you use, you must have checked every source yourself and be able to explain any entry in a short follow-up conversation if the tutor asks.
