# GS101 · Final essay brief

**Weight:** 35% of the course grade, marked out of 100.
**Work mode:** individual.
**Length:** 2,800 to 3,200 words, excluding tables, figures, appendices and the source list.
**Presentation:** week 15, eight minutes plus a five-minute viva.
**Due:** the written essay is due at the end of week 15, after your presentation, so you can use the feedback.
**Course outcomes it assesses:** all three. Place games on a timeline with causes, write a critical reading, and explain how technology, business and culture shaped design.

## The task

Write a critical essay that answers one question about how games changed, using the history and the critical tools of the whole course. The essay joins the two kinds of work you have done this term. The timeline project asked "why did this happen when it did?" The close readings asked "what does this game's design do and mean?" The final essay asks both about one topic.

### Choosing a question

A good question crosses at least two eras and can be answered with evidence you can check. Some patterns that work:

- **One design problem across eras.** "How have games priced the moral choice, from The Landlord's Game to a live-service game?" (weeks 1, 12, 13)
- **One business force across eras.** "How has the way players pay shaped session length, from coin-op to free-to-play?" (weeks 3, 9, 10, 11)
- **One game or series across versions.** "What changed between the original release and the modern re-release of [game], and which of those changes follow from hardware, business or audience?" (weeks 8, 10)
- **One constraint turned into style.** "When did a technical limit become a genre's identity?" (weeks 2, 5, 8)
- **Representation over time.** "Has [group or role] become better represented in [genre], compared to what?" (weeks 13, 14)

You may continue the topic of one of your close readings or your timeline commentary, but the essay must add at least one new game and one new era.

## What the essay must contain

1. **A question and a one-paragraph answer** at the start. The answer is your thesis. It should be possible for a classmate to disagree with it.
2. **A historical argument.** A dated chain across at least two eras, drawn from your timeline project and extended where needed. Name mechanisms, not only events, and give the strongest alternative cause for at least one link.
3. **At least two course models or frameworks, applied with numbers.** One must be quantitative (for example `1 / L`, the shelf model, `N*`, the d20 staircase, the polygon budget, `S`, `C` and `W`, `N × p × A`, the budget, the ledger ratios, or the representation index). The other may be a critical framework (Caillois's categories, procedural rhetoric, Jenkins's narrative architecture, Juul's half-real, the auteur lens used critically). State every assumption and every estimated number.
4. **At least one close reading passage** of a specific moment in a game you played for this essay, with your play log in an appendix.
5. **A counter-argument section.** The strongest objection to your thesis and your answer to it, plus what your evidence cannot show.
6. **Sources.** At least eight, including at least two scholarly or book sources (the course readings count: Donovan's *Replay*, Bartle's *Designing Virtual Worlds*, Caillois, Juul, Bogost, Jenkins, the virtual census papers). Every game fact cites a source with version or era. Company figures, press reports and your own estimates are labelled as such.

## The presentation and viva (week 15)

- **Eight minutes.** Your question, your thesis, one figure or table that carries the argument, and the counter-argument. Slides are optional; if you use them, no game logos, box art or screenshots.
- **Five-minute viva.** The assessor asks two questions adapted from the "apply" questions in the [exam bank](exam-bank.md), with your essay game in place of the question's case. The viva prompts at the end of the bank show the pattern. Expect one question on a model and one on your sources or method.

The presentation is a draft stage. Use the feedback to finish the written essay by the end of the week.

## Rubric for the presentation and viva (20 points)

| Criterion | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Argument** (6) | 6: Question, thesis and counter-argument clear within eight minutes | 5: Clear; one part rushed | 3-4: Thesis unclear or over time by more than two minutes | 0-2: No thesis |
| **Evidence shown** (4) | 4: One figure or table carries the argument, with its source and scale labelled | 3: Evidence shown but not tied to the thesis | 2: Evidence thin | 0-1: None |

## Rubric for the final essay (65 points)

| Criterion | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Question and thesis** (10) | 9-10: A specific question answered in the first paragraph; the thesis is disputable and every section serves it | 7-8: Clear thesis; one section drifts | 5-6: Question broad or thesis stated only at the end | 0-4: No question; a survey or review |
| **Historical argument** (15) | 14-15: A dated chain across two or more eras; mechanisms named; one alternative cause weighed; evidence types kept apart | 11-13: Chain clear; mechanisms or alternative thin in places | 8-10: Chronology with little causation, or one era only | 0-7: No historical argument, or dates wrong |
| **Models and frameworks** (15) | 14-15: Two models that fit the question; numbers worked and checkable; assumptions stated; the essay says where each model breaks | 11-13: Both models correct and relevant; assumptions partly stated | 8-10: One model only, or models applied with errors | 0-7: No model, or lecture numbers copied without adaptation |
| **Close reading and evidence from play** (10) | 9-10: A specific moment read closely; play log cited by session and timestamp; rules and fiction both considered | 7-8: Close reading present but brief | 5-6: Play evidence thin or decorative | 0-4: No play evidence |
| **Counter-argument and limits** (10) | 9-10: The strongest objection stated fairly and answered; limits of the evidence named | 7-8: Objection present; answer thin | 5-6: A weak objection chosen, or limits missing | 0-4: None |
| **Sources and writing** (5) | 5: Eight or more sources, two scholarly; every fact versioned; plain prose within the word range | 4: One or two lapses | 3: Several lapses, or under eight sources | 0-2: Unsourced facts, invented quotes, or outside the word range by more than 10% |

Total: 5 (M1) + 10 (M2) + 20 (presentation and viva) + 65 (essay) = 100.

## Rules

- **One question.** An essay that answers three questions answers none of them well.
- **No invented data.** Every number in your play log must come from a session that happened. Label estimates as estimates and give their assumptions.
- **Your own words.** Paraphrase and cite. Quote at most one short phrase from any one source.
- **No copyrighted images** in the essay or the slides. Describe what you saw.
- **AI and writing tools** follow the program's current policy. Whatever tools you use, the argument, play evidence and calculations must be yours. The viva exists partly to check that you can explain them.
