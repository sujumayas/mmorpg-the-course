# GS101 · Close readings brief

**Weight:** 40% of the course grade, as two close readings worth 20% each. Each is marked out of 100.
**Work mode:** individual.
**Length:** 1,200 to 1,500 words each, excluding tables, the play log appendix and the source list.
**Due:** close reading 1 at the end of week 10. Close reading 2 at the end of week 14.
**Course outcome it assesses:** write a critical close reading of a game.

## The task

A close reading is a careful analysis of how one specific part of a game produces its effect on the player, supported by evidence from play and placed in its historical context. It makes one claim a reader could dispute. It is not a review, and "is it good?" is not the question.

Both readings use the method built up over the course:

1. **Describe before you judge** (week 1). Components, rules, win condition, and which parts you know from the game, a source or your own inference.
2. **Find the machine** (week 1, sharpened in week 12). One rule or system, worked out with numbers.
3. **Name the experience** (week 1). Use a vocabulary, such as Caillois's categories or procedural rhetoric.
4. **Check the layers** (week 13, required in close reading 2 only). The rules-fiction ledger for one choice the fiction cares about.
5. **Place it in context** (week 1). Who made it, for whom, on what hardware, under what business model, in what year.
6. **Make one claim** (week 1). One sentence someone could argue against.

## Close reading 1: a game from before 2003

Choose a game first released before 2003 that you can play for at least two hours (an original release, an official re-release, or a legal emulator with a copy you own), or a parser game you can play in full. Name the version you played, because re-releases change draw distance, frame rate and sometimes rules.

Your "find the machine" step must use one model from weeks 1 to 9, applied to your game with numbers:

- a randomizer and scoring rule (week 1);
- an update loop and `accel` rule (week 2);
- the quarter clock, `1 / L` and a difficulty ramp (week 3);
- the shelf model, if your reading concerns a game's release context (week 4);
- the parser's limits or the shared-treasure crossover `N*` (week 5);
- a d20 or smooth hit formula with its clamps (week 6);
- the mod pipeline lags (week 7);
- the three-step constraint reading with a polygon budget (week 8);
- the `S`, `C`, `W` model for an early online game (week 9).

Practice runs: [Lab 01](../labs/lab01.md), [Lab 05](../labs/lab05.md) and [Lab 08](../labs/lab08.md). You may develop one of them into close reading 1, but the graded piece must be at least 50% new writing and use a longer play log.

## Close reading 2: a game that argues through its rules

Choose any game, from a different decade than close reading 1. Your reading must include the "check the layers" step and one of these two tools, applied with numbers:

- the "find the budget" method from week 12 (scarce resource, income rule, needs, margin, price of the moral choice); or
- the rules-fiction ledger from week 13, with both `I_encounter` and `I_game`.

You may also use the representation index from week 14 as a third lens, as long as the reading stays focused on one claim.

Practice runs: [Lab 11](../labs/lab11.md), [Lab 12](../labs/lab12.md) and [Lab 13](../labs/lab13.md). Lab 12 is designed as a draft of this piece.

## What each reading must contain

1. **A claim in the first paragraph.** For example, "Kendall's jackpot rule shifts Senet from agon toward alea" (week 1) or "In this game's world, mercy costs a day's food, so the rules make kindness a family decision" (week 12). "This is an interesting game" is not a claim.
2. **A concrete moment from your play.** Open with it or build to it. Cite your play log by session and timestamp.
3. **One table of computed numbers** from the game's rules or from your model, with one row's arithmetic shown in full.
4. **Context that explains the machine.** Hardware, business model, team, audience or era, used to explain why the rule looks the way it does. The auteur lens belongs here, not in step 2 (week 12).
5. **A counter-reading.** The strongest reading that disagrees with yours, and why yours holds up.
6. **Sources.** Every fact about the game's rules, numbers or development cites a source with version or era. Say whether a number is shown by the game, documented by a source, or measured by you.
7. **A play log appendix.** At least two sessions, dated, with what you did and what the game showed.

## Rubric for each final reading (95 points)

The same rubric marks both readings. The "required lens" row changes: for CR1 it is a model from weeks 1 to 9; for CR2 it is the "check the layers" step plus the budget or the ledger.

| Criterion | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Claim** (15) | 14-15: One specific, disputable claim in the first paragraph; every section serves it | 11-13: Clear claim; one section drifts | 8-10: Claim broad, or stated only at the end | 0-7: No claim; description or review |
| **Evidence from play** (15) | 14-15: Two or more logged sessions; a concrete moment analysed closely; log cited by timestamp | 11-13: Play evidence present; one claim rests on memory | 8-10: Log thin or used only as decoration | 0-7: No play log, or reading built from reviews and videos |
| **Machine and computation** (20) | 18-20: The rule is stated exactly with version; the model fits the question; the table recomputes; one row shown in full; the reading says where the model stops | 14-17: Correct and relevant; one assumption unstated | 10-13: Numbers present with errors, or not linked to the claim | 0-9: No computation, or lecture numbers copied without adaptation |
| **Required lens** (15) | 14-15: CR1: the chosen model applied to this game with assumptions labelled. CR2: ledger or budget complete, both spans counted, classification justified, intent discussed | 11-13: Lens applied; one part thin (for example `I_game` missing) | 8-10: Lens named but applied loosely | 0-7: Lens missing |
| **Context** (15) | 14-15: Context explains why the rule looks as it does; business model, hardware or team named with sources; auteur claims handled critically | 11-13: Context relevant but only partly tied to the rule | 8-10: Context present but decorative | 0-7: No context, or invented motives presented as fact |
| **Counter-reading and sources** (10) | 9-10: A fair counter-reading answered; every fact sourced with version; shown, documented and measured values kept apart | 7-8: Counter-reading brief, or one fact unsourced | 5-6: No counter-reading, or several unsourced facts | 0-4: Facts wrong, invented quotes, or no sources |
| **Writing** (5) | 5: Plain and precise, within the word range, tables where they help | 4: Readable, some padding | 3: Hard to follow in places | 0-2: Outside the word range by more than 10%, or hard to read |

Each reading: 5 (proposal) + 95 (final) = 100. The course grade uses `0.2 × CR1 + 0.2 × CR2`.

## Rules

- **One game per reading, two different games.** They may not be the same game or from the same decade.
- **Not a lecture's main worked example as your whole reading.** You may read Papers, Please or BioShock, but you must study a different resource, choice or stretch of the game than the lecture did, as [Lab 12](../labs/lab12.md) explains.
- **No invented data.** Every number in your play log must come from a session that happened. Label estimates as estimates.
- **Your own words.** Paraphrase and cite. Quote at most one short phrase from any one source. Transcript lines from a parser game you played are play evidence and may be quoted.
- **No game art in the submission.** Describe what you saw in words with a timestamp.
- **AI and writing tools** follow the program's current policy. Whatever tools you use, the play evidence and calculations must be yours, and you must be able to explain any line in a short follow-up conversation if the tutor asks. Tutors may use the [exam bank](exam-bank.md) questions in that conversation.
