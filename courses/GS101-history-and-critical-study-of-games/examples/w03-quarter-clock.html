The quarter clock

An arcade machine earns p × u × 60 / L dollars an hour, so halving the session length L doubles the money. A difficulty ramp is how designers cut L, and it cuts the experts' long sessions far more than the novices' short ones.

Model from the GS101 week 3 lecture: session L(S) = min(30, max(0.5, (S − d₀) / r)); plays/day = u × H × 60 / L; revenue/hour = p × u × 60 / L; operator/day = s × revenue/hour × H; payback = cabinet / operator/day. The Pong calibration (p = $0.25, u = 0.75, H = 10, L = 3) is one invented combination that reproduces Bushnell's reported $35 to $40 a day. It is not a measurement of any real bar. Cabinet price $2,400 is a golden-age (about 1981) figure.
