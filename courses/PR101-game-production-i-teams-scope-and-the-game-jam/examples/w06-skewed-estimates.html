PR101 Game Production I · Week 6 · Worked example

The typical task is on time. The plan is late.

A task can only come in a little early, but it can run a lot late. On a log scale the ratios look balanced around 1. Added up in hours, the late side wins, and the plan ratio follows G × eσ²/2, not the median.

Spread explorer: synthetic tasks with median 1

Every task is estimated at 2 h and takes 2 × eσz h, with z drawn from a seeded normal distribution (shifted so its median is 0). Moving a slider shows the synthetic plan in both views above.

The preloaded 120 rows are invented for teaching (labs/lab06-starter/jam_plans_example.csv). They describe no real cohort. Columns: team,rule,task,estimate_h,actual_h. A blank actual_h means the task was cut and is not scored. You can also paste two columns, estimate and actual.

Formulas from the week 6 lecture. r = actual ÷ estimate for finished tasks. Percentiles use the linear method (h = (n − 1)p). σ is the population standard deviation of ln r, G = emean ln r, R = Σ actual ÷ Σ estimate. The default data is invented and describes no real team.
