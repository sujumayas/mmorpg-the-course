# MMO403 · Ethics analysis brief

**Weight:** 20% of the course grade.
**Work mode:** the lab pieces follow each lab's rules; the synthesis report is individual.
**Due:** lab pieces with their labs; the synthesis report at the end of week 12.

## What the ethics analysis is

This course asks one question in many forms: where does monetization stop and manipulation start? The ethics analysis is your answer, built in two layers.

1. **Five lab pieces** from weeks 3 to 8. Each applies one lens to a store or a design, with numbers from that week's model.
2. **A synthesis report** on one real live game, written after week 11, that combines the lenses into one judgment and one set of recommendations with their revenue cost.

The point is not to condemn or defend games. A good analysis names what the studio gains from a design, what players pay for it in money, time or trust, what the law says as of a stated date, and what a better version would cost. It is balanced because the numbers make it so, not because you split the difference.

## The synthesis report

### Task

Choose one live game with a store that you can document from public sources: store pages, patch notes, official announcements, rating certificates and maintained wikis. It may be one you used in a lab. Write an ethics analysis of its monetization for its studio's leadership, of at most 1,800 words plus tables.

### Deliverables

1. **The report** (PDF, at most 1,800 words, tables and figures not counted). It must contain:
   - **The store as a player meets it.** What is sold, in which currency, at what real price, with dates and sources. Describe screens in words. Do not paste game art, logos or screenshots.
   - **At least four lenses from weeks 3 to 9,** each with a number from the lecture model: forced leftover or markup (week 3), rating-point value of any paid edge (week 4), finish share or near-miss band of a pass (week 5), median and 90th-percentile cost of a random item (week 6), outcome in the eight markets (week 7), a wall's LTV against lifetime or a flow's asymmetry ratio (week 8), or concentration and the cost of a spending protection (week 9). Where you lack the studio's data, state your assumption and use the model for direction and scale, not as a claim about the real figure.
   - **The regulatory position** of the game's paid random rewards and currency practices, stated "as of" a date, with primary sources where the lecture gives them. If anything has changed since 28 September 2026, cite the change.
   - **The other side.** What each practice does for the studio and for players: funding a free game, pacing content, unifying prices, offering choice. At least one paragraph argues the studio's case as its designers would.
   - **Recommendations.** Two to four changes, each with its estimated revenue cost from a lecture model and the player group it protects. At least one must be cheap.
2. **An appendix** with every calculation (code or a formula sheet) and a source list with read dates.
3. **A one-paragraph summary** at the top that a producer could read in one minute.

## Rules

- **Paraphrase and cite.** Quote at most one short phrase per source. Name games in text only.
- **Date every rule and price.** Platform terms, store prices and loot box law change. A statement without a date is marked as undated.
- **Model, not legal advice.** Any compliance conclusion must say that it comes from the course's model of the rules, not from a lawyer.
- **No personal data.** Do not collect data from players or scrape accounts. Public store and policy pages are enough.
- **Late work.** Lab pieces follow the lab's deadline. The synthesis report loses 5 points per day late for up to five days, after which it scores zero unless an extension was agreed before the deadline.
