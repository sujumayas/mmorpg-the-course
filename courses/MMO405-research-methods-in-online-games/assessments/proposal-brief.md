# MMO405 · Proposal brief: research proposal and ethics application

**Weight:** 20% of the course grade.
**Due:** final proposal at the end of week 12. Three earlier milestones are required but not graded (see "Milestones").
**Work mode:** individual. A pair may write one proposal for a joint paper if the instructor approves it by the end of week 2; each partner then adds a half-page contribution statement.
**Labs that feed it:** labs 01, 03 to 08, 10 and 12. The table in "Structure" names which lab feeds which section.
**Related briefs:** [methods-labs-brief.md](methods-labs-brief.md) and [paper-brief.md](paper-brief.md). The paper reports the study this proposal plans.

## What the proposal is for

The course has two outcomes: design an ethical study of an online game, and combine quantitative and qualitative methods. The proposal is where you do the first and plan the second. It is the plan for the study you will report in your final paper, written so that three readers can act on it.

- **Your instructor**, who decides whether the study is feasible in one term and which ethics route it takes.
- **An ethics reviewer**, who decides whether players are safe in it. The ethics section is a complete application in the form taught in [week 10](../lectures/w10-ethics-and-irb.md).
- **A future reader of your paper**, who will compare what you did with what you said you would do. Treat the proposal like the first stage of a registered report ([week 13](../lectures/w13-replication.md)): the question, design, measures and analysis plan are fixed before you see the outcome data.

## Choosing a data route

Every study takes one of four routes. You name your route at milestone 1, and the instructor confirms it.

| Route | What it covers | Ethics step before any data | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| S, secondary | Existing data with no interaction: public aggregate statistics, a public API used within its terms of service, a published dataset, or data a studio shares under a written agreement | A determination that the study is not human-subjects research, or is exempt secondary research under §46.104(d)(4), confirmed by the instructor or your institution's review office | Class-pick shares from a public statistics page; a released match dataset |
| P, course protocol | Minimal-risk studies with adults that fit the course's standing protocol: anonymous surveys on non-sensitive topics, unrecorded or note-only interviews on non-sensitive topics, open-role observation of public spaces as in [lab 07](../labs/lab07.md) | Your protocol signed off by the instructor at milestone 2 | A 10-minute survey on guild belonging; field notes in a city hub |
| R, full review | Anything outside route P: identifiable data linked to accounts, recorded interviews, sensitive topics such as rule-breaking or harassment, deception, any participant under 18 | Written approval from your institution's board or committee | A survey linked to telemetry; interviews about harassment |
| M, modelled | A full design whose analysis runs on simulated data plus a role-play pilot with classmates using invented personas, as in [lab 06](../labs/lab06.md) | None beyond the course rules, because no real player data is collected | A fallback when route R approval does not arrive in time |

The instructor gives you the reference number of the course's standing protocol in week 1. If your institution does not run one, every route P study becomes a route R study, and you should plan for that at milestone 1.

A route R study that has no approval by the end of week 11 switches to route M for the paper, without penalty, and the proposal stays as written. You then report the design, the pilot and the modelled analysis, and describe the approved study as future work.

## Structure

Length: 3,000 to 4,500 words for sections 1 to 8, plus appendices. Appendices do not count toward the word limit.

| Section | Contents | Draws on | Lab that feeds it | Target words |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2. Background and gap | Two to four prior findings that bear on the question, with their designs and effect sizes, and the reason they do not answer it. A line of argument, not a list. | Weeks 1, 2 and 11 | | 500 |
| 3. Design | The design on the week 2 map and its strongest claim type. For a mixed study, Morse notation, the purpose in Greene and colleagues' terms, and the integration step (connecting, building, merging or embedding). | 8. Timeline and risks | Week-by-week plan from sign-off to the week 15 paper. The two risks most likely to stop the study and what you will do if each happens, including the route M fallback. | All | | 200 |

**Appendices.**

- **A. Instruments.** Survey items with response scales, the interview guide, the recording plan template, or the telemetry variable list.
- **B. Participant materials.** Recruitment text exactly as it will appear, the consent page or form, the debrief if any, and the distress protocol card.
- **C. Preregistration.** A time-stamped copy of the analysis plan from section 6 (an OSF or AsPredicted registration, or the milestone 2 submission as time-stamped by the course platform).
- **D. Response letter.** Your point-by-point answer to the classmate's review from [lab 12](../labs/lab12.md), with page references to what changed.

## Ethics and consent requirements

Section 7 follows the application template in [week 10](../lectures/w10-ethics-and-irb.md), section 3. A proposal that leaves out any required item below cannot be approved for data collection. Items marked "if applicable" can be answered with one sentence saying why they do not apply.

1. **Classification.** Is the study human-subjects research under §46.102(e)(1) and (l)? Give its review level (not human-subjects research, exempt with the category from §46.104(d)(2) to (d)(4), expedited, or full board) and name the fact that decides it. If you study in the UK or EU, also name the committee that would review it.
2. **Adults only, with a screen.** Say how you exclude players under 18: an age question at the start that ends the survey or session, plus recruitment in adult spaces. Admit that the screen is imperfect. A study that needs minors is route R and requires parental permission and assent.
3. **Recruitment.** The exact text, where it will appear, and who posts it. If a guild leader or moderator shares it, ask them to post it neutrally and without endorsing it, and add a second route that does not pass through them ([week 6](../lectures/w06-qualitative-methods-interviews.md)). Say what incentive you offer, and confirm that anyone who withdraws partway still receives it.
4. **Consent, key information first.** Five plain sentences at the top: who you are, what you ask, how long it takes, the main risk, and that the participant can stop at any time without losing anything (§46.116(a)(5)(i)). Then the full elements of §46.116(b). If you record audio or video, ask for recording consent separately from interview consent. If you link survey answers to logs, the consent must say what data will be linked, for what purpose and for how long ([week 3](../lectures/w03-telemetry-as-research-data.md)). Give the date after which data can no longer be withdrawn, and the reason.
5. **Waivers (if applicable).** A waiver of signed documentation (an "I agree" page) is argued under §46.117(c). A waiver or alteration of consent itself is argued against each of the five criteria of §46.116(f)(3), one paragraph per criterion, with the debrief text if you withhold information.
6. **Public and private spaces.** For observation or collection of player text, place each space on the private-information test of §46.102(e)(4) and the AoIR context test ([week 7](../lectures/w07-ethnography-and-participant-observation.md)). Say what software you will run and whether the game's terms of service allow it.
7. **Identifiability.** Pseudonymize people, characters, guilds and servers. Say how you will paraphrase quotes from public sources. For network data, state how you will handle third parties who did not consent and how you will prevent structural re-identification ([week 9](../lectures/w09-social-network-analysis-of-guilds.md)).
8. **Data management.** What you collect, where it is stored, who can see it, when it is pseudonymized, who holds the key and when the key is destroyed, and a deletion date. If EU or UK players are involved, name the GDPR lawful basis separately from ethical consent and list the Article 89(1) safeguards you apply (week 3). If a studio holds the data, say who controls publication.
9. **Distress and incidental findings.** A written protocol: what you will say, which resources you will give (a crisis line for the participant's country, the game's own reporting tools), when you stop a session, and whom you tell. List the incidental findings you might meet (cheating in logs, a minor, a plan to harass someone), what you will do with each, and the promise you make in the consent form about them. Do not promise confidentiality you cannot keep.
10. **Sensitive questions (if applicable).** If you ask about anything a participant could be banned, shamed or harmed for admitting, say whether you considered randomized response or an item count, and why you did or did not use it. If you use randomized response, give `t`, the implied `ε` and the sample size from the week 10 model.
11. **The double problem.** One feature of your design that is both an ethical problem and a validity problem, and the change that fixes both ([lab 10](../labs/lab10.md), step 8).

**No data from real players before sign-off.** Route P studies may start collecting only after the instructor signs the milestone 2 protocol. Route R studies may start only after written approval from the board. Collecting data before that point scores 0 on criterion G below and on the paper's ethics criterion, and the data cannot be used in the paper.

## Milestones

Deviations from the protocol after sign-off are allowed. Record each one, with its date and reason, in a deviations log. The paper reports the log in its method section.

## Rubric (100 points)

The instructor marks against the bands below. A missing section scores 0 on its criterion.

| Criterion | Points | Excellent | Good | Adequate | Weak or missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Question and claim type (sections 1 and 2) | 10 | 9-10: all eleven card fields precise; claim type matches the data the route can give; FINER scores justified; background argues toward a stated gap with prior effect sizes | 7-8: one field vague, or background partly a list | 5-6: claim type stronger than the design supports, or gap not stated | 0-4: topic without a question |
| B. Design and validity (section 3) | 15 | 13-15: design placed on the map; for mixed studies, notation, purpose and a named integration step; each remaining threat paired with a check | 10-12: threats named without checks, or integration step vague | 7-9: design named but threats generic | 0-6: no design argument |
| C. Data and measures (section 4) | 15 | 13-15: every construct operationalized; telemetry rules, scale sources and reliability plan, guide or recording plan complete for the strands used; direction of each cleaning bias stated | 10-12: one strand thinly specified | 7-9: measures named without rules or sources | 0-6: missing |
| D. Sample and power (section 5) | 15 | 13-15: SESOI justified before data; sample size calculated from it; recruitment bias named with a second route; qualitative selection rule written in advance with contrast cases | 10-12: calculation present but SESOI weakly justified | 7-9: sample stated without a calculation or rule | 0-6: missing |
| E. Analysis plan (section 6) | 15 | 13-15: primary outcome, exclusions, model and sensitivity analyses fixed and time-stamped; exploratory analyses marked; IRR decision argued; empty joint display drawn | 10-12: one element missing | 7-9: plan too vague to hold you to it | 0-6: missing |
| F. Feasibility and response to review (section 8, appendix D) | 10 | 9-10: realistic timeline with fallback; every review comment answered with a located change or an argued refusal | 7-8: timeline tight without a fallback, or some comments unanswered | 5-6: response letter generic | 0-4: missing |
| G. Ethics application (section 7) | 20 | 18-20: all eleven items present; classification cited to the regulation; key-information consent in five plain sentences; waivers argued criterion by criterion; identifiability, data dates and distress protocol concrete; a real double problem fixed on both sides | 14-17: one item thin, or one waiver criterion unargued | 10-13: several items generic; classification uncited | 0-9: items missing, or the plan would expose participants |

A proposal that scores below 10 on criterion G must be revised and approved before you report any participant data in the paper. The revision does not change the proposal's grade.

## Late work and extensions

- The final proposal may be submitted up to 72 hours late with a penalty of 5 points per 24 hours. After 72 hours it scores 0, and you must still submit it before collecting data.
- Extensions are granted for documented illness or emergency. A late ethics decision from a board is not your fault: tell the instructor, and switch to route M if needed.

## Academic integrity

Discuss your idea with anyone, and use the lab 12 review. The proposal must be your own writing. Reusing your own lab text is expected; mark it with a footnote naming the lab. Reusing a pair lab's shared work is allowed if you name the partner and the table. If you use a generative AI tool, say how, and verify every citation and number yourself. Never paste a classmate's draft or any participant data into an online tool, because that shares them ([week 12](../lectures/w12-peer-review.md), confidentiality).
