Alpha says “good”. The items say two scales.

Cronbach's alpha only sees the average correlation and the number of items. Twelve items in two halves that share nothing still score .818. Push the item count up and the gauge reaches “excellent” while the two blocks stay exactly as separate.

Model: k items in two groups of h = k/2. Within pairs W = 2 × h(h − 1)/2, between pairs B = h², r̄ = (W·rw + B·rb) / (k(k − 1)/2), α = k·r̄ / (1 + (k − 1)·r̄), half-sum correlation = h²·rb / (h + h(h − 1)·rw). Bands are the common rule of thumb quoted in the lecture: below .60 poor, .60 questionable, .70 acceptable, .80 good, .90 excellent.
