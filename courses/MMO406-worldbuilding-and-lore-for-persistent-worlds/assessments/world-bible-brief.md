# MMO406 · World bible brief

**Weight:** 60% of the course grade, marked out of 100.
**Work mode:** individual. Askers for your question tests and readers for your listening and reconstruction tests can be anyone. The world, the writing and the numbers are your own.
**Assigned:** week 1. **Final submission:** week 15, day 1. **Defence:** week 15, days 2 to 5.
**Milestones that count toward it:** foundations draft (end of week 5), systems draft (end of week 10) and the workshop draft (start of week 14).
**Lectures:** the whole course. The table in "What the bible must contain" names the week and lab behind each requirement.

## What this component is for

This is the course's final project: a world bible for an original persistent online world. It tests both course outcomes together. You create a setting bible that scales across expansions, and you tie its lore to the game's systems, so that factions, economy and geography keep producing stories after the launch story is spent.

Judge the bible the way [week 14](../lectures/w14-bible-workshop.md) does, as a tool. A writer who joins your team in year three opens it with a question. The bible is done when three things are true. A new writer can answer most of their questions from it without asking you. A marker can check every number in it by running your lab tools on your appendices. And nothing in it contradicts anything else in it.

## What you make

A bible for one world, the world you started in [lab 01](../labs/lab01.md) and grew through every lab since. You choose the genre, tone and scale. Everything is your own invention.

Hand in four things.

1. **The bible document.** 12,000 to 18,000 words of main text, not counting tables, appendices and data files. It opens with a one-page front of at most 500 words.
2. **The appendices.** Every data file your lab tools read (the ledger JSON, region and recipe files, faction web, timeline, syllable inventories, fact matrix, beat table, choices file, realm file, plan file, canon file, transmedia plan, day file), with the final output of each tool pasted in.
3. **The question logs.** Both question tests from [lab 14](../labs/lab14.md), as CSV files, with the askers' writing tasks.
4. **A marker's readme.** At most one page. How to run each tool on each appendix, and the one number each run should print.

### Originality rule

The world is yours. Every setting, faction, place, character, god, language, calendar and line of lore is your own invention.

- You may not base the world on the setting of any existing game, book, film, television series, tabletop game or fan work. A world that renames another setting's factions and keeps their history fails this rule.
- You may not use *Thornmere*, the course's sample world, or its characters.
- Do not copy lore text from any shipped game, wiki or novel, not even as a placeholder or an epigraph. At most one short quoted phrase from a real source, cited, in an analysis section.
- Real games may appear only in analysis, where you compare a decision in your bible with one the lectures covered. Describe the case in your own words and cite the lecture.
- Borrowing structure from real cultures, languages and religions follows [week 5](../lectures/w05-cultures-languages-names.md) and [week 6](../lectures/w06-religion-and-myth-in-fiction.md). Borrow on purpose and from more than one source. Never make a living faith's sacred figures, texts or symbols into villains or jokes.

Markers compare names, factions and history against well-known settings. A bible that fails the rule scores 0 on "Writing and originality" and is referred under the academic integrity policy.

### A sample reserved-list entry

This sample is invented to show the format. Your entries will be about your own world.

> **R04. Who sank the lighthouse fleet?** Linked facts: H12 (the fleet sank in the year 611), H13 (no wreck has been found). Hook H3, vague, planted at launch, target payoff expansion 3, `d = 3`, `d* = 3.98` at `ρ = 0.2`. Writers may mention the loss and the grief. Do not name a culprit, a cause or a survivor. Ask the lore lead.

## Defence

Twenty minutes, with two examiners and your bible open on screen.

1. **The pitch (5 minutes).** Your world in the words of your one-page front, and the one system-backed structural tension you think will last longest, with its `g`.
2. **A live change (8 minutes).** The examiners propose one change to your world: flip a faction edge, move a date, cancel an outside medium, shorten an expansion cycle or merge two realms. Before you run anything, predict the direction of two outputs and say which model tells you. Then run the tools on your appendices and report what moved, including the ripple into other chapters.
3. **An exam-bank question (5 minutes).** One question from the [exam bank](exam-bank.md), rewritten with your bible's numbers. Answer it with the model named.
4. **Your weakest chapter (2 minutes).** Name it, give the number that shows it is weak, and say what you would fix next.

## How the numbers are checked

Markers run your lab tools on your appendices, using your readme. Every number in the bible must match the tool's output to the precision printed. A number that does not match, or that has no appendix behind it, scores as missing for its chapter. If you changed a tool after its lab was marked, say what you changed in the readme and keep its tests passing.

## Late work

The final submission loses 5 of its 70 points per 24 hours late, up to the day before your defence. Nothing is accepted after that, and the defence goes ahead on whatever was submitted. Milestones lose 1 point per 48 hours late. The free extensions from the [exercises brief](exercises-brief.md) do not apply to the bible.

## Academic integrity

The bible is individual. You may discuss ideas and test each other's bibles, which the course requires. You may not share data files, chapters or tool output. All lore must meet the originality rule. Every fact about a shipped game must come from a course lecture and cite it. Invented or altered question logs, listening tests, reconstruction tests or tool output score 0 for the affected criteria and are treated as misconduct.
