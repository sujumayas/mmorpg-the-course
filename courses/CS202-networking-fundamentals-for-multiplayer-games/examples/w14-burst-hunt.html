Hunting a bug that needs three lost packets in a row

Same 1% loss. Bursty finds the bug in about a minute. Independent needs more than a day.

—loss rate p, and q = P(loss | previous lost)
—mean burst 1 / (1 − q)
—a = p(1 − q) / (1 − p), P(loss | previous delivered)
—β = p(1 − q)qk−1, bursts ≥ k per packet
—λ = 60·r·β, bursts per minute
—mean wait 1 / λ
—95% found by, ln 20 / λ
—P(found in a 10-minute playtest)

Model: week 14 lecture, section 2. Two-state (Gilbert) loss: the first packet follows a delivered one, and each packet is lost with probability q after a loss and a otherwise. The strip uses the lecture's GilbertLoss dropper with the same Mersenne Twister as Python's random.Random(seed), so seed 14 here gives the same packets as the lab. Minute m is packets 60·r·(m − 1) to 60·r·m − 1 of one stream. Detection treats burst starts as a Poisson process with rate λ, which is close when bursts are rare per minute.
