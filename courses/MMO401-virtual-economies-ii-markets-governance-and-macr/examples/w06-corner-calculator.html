A corner pays only if supply takes longer than L* to return

A trader buys the whole ask ladder of a reagent and holds the price up until fresh supply arrives. Every defensive lever works by raising the break-even lag L* or by shortening the real lag.

Model from the week 6 lecture. The trader buys the whole ladder, sells a(t) = max(0, a − bP − s) units a day at the hold price for L days, then sells what is left at Pafter. L* is computed exactly as the smallest lag with best profit ≥ 0, and "never" if it exceeds 60 days. The reagent, the ladders and the demand curve are invented for teaching.
