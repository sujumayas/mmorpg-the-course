# The Keeper of Saltmere (invented lore dump for Lab 10)

This is the text a quest giver currently reads out, in one panel, when the player accepts "Light the Saltmere Lamp". It is deliberately bad. All names are invented for this course.

Saltmere was founded nine generations ago by the Tallow Families, who fled the inland wars with nothing but their boats and a stubborn belief that the sea would feed them. For two hundred years the town grew rich on herring and on the salt pans that give it its name. The lighthouse on Gull Tooth rock was built in the town's second century by the Brotherhood of the Wick, an order of lamp-keepers who swore never to let the light go dark. Each keeper served for exactly seven years, and each left a verse carved into the stair rail. The last keeper, a woman named Ide Marrow, broke the oath eleven years ago when she climbed down during the Long Storm to save her drowning brother. That night, with the lamp dark, the grain fleet from Corvel ran onto the Tooth and forty sailors died. Corvel has never forgiven Saltmere, and its merchants have refused to trade here ever since. The Brotherhood expelled Ide, and she now lives alone in the drowned quarter, below the high-tide line. The town council blames the Brotherhood, the Brotherhood blames Ide, and Ide blames no one but herself. Since that night nobody has been willing to climb Gull Tooth, because the townsfolk say the forty drowned sailors walk the stair. The truth is less romantic: the stair is rotten, and three people have fallen from it. The lamp itself still works, and its oil store in the cellar is full. To relight it, someone needs the brass wick key, which the Brotherhood took with them when they left. The Brotherhood's last house is the chapel on the cliff road, north of the harbor. The current prior, Brother Anselm, is Ide's cousin and has not spoken to her since the expulsion. He will only hand over the key to someone who brings word from Ide, because he believes the light belongs to her. Ide, for her part, will only give that word to someone who has read her brother's verse on the stair rail. Her brother, Tam Marrow, was a keeper's apprentice who carved one line before he drowned. You can reach the stair from the causeway at low tide, which comes twice a day. Be careful on the rotten steps above the fourth landing. When the lamp is lit again, Corvel's ships may return, and Saltmere may finally be able to grieve.
