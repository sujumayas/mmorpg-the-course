Why n = 5 lies

One playtest of 5 players gives one tidy interval. Run the same playtest 100 times and the simple 95% interval misses the true clear rate about 16 times, not 5. The orange bars on the right are the misses.

Model: each player clears with probability p, independently, so k counts uniform draws below p. Simple (Wald): p̂ ± 1.96 × sqrt(p̂(1 − p̂)/n). Wilson: center (p̂ + z²/2n)/(1 + z²/n), half-width z × sqrt(p̂(1 − p̂)/n + z²/4n²)/(1 + z²/n), z = 1.96. Coverage is exact: the sum of binomial probabilities of every k whose interval contains p, computed in log space. Random draws come from mulberry32; with Fixed seed on, the first screen uses seed 13 and is the same for every student. See Agresti and Coull (1998), "Approximate is better than exact for interval estimation of binomial proportions".
